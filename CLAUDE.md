# rpg-ai

A 2D open-world RPG where all AI runs locally. NPCs generate dialogue via an LLM, quests are created dynamically from conversations, and the world context is AI-generated at startup.

## How to run

```bash
uv run game
```

Requires CUDA drivers and the model at `models/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Q2_K.gguf`. See README for setup.

## File map

One line per file. Update this when adding, removing, or substantially repurposing a file, this list is what keeps lookups fast instead of requiring a codebase search.

`saves/save.json`: persisted game state (gitignored). `models/`: GGUF model files (gitignored).

### rpg_ai
- `src/rpg_ai/__main__.py`: entry point. Initialises Pygame and the LLM queue, then loops from main menu to game, building a fresh `SaveSystem` per session (never shared with the previous game, whose generation threads may still be finishing) and clearing it on "New Game"; exits when `Game.quit_app` says the window was closed; the window opens fullscreen through `pygame.SCALED`, which keeps the fixed layout resolution and translates mouse coordinates for it

### game
- `src/game/game.py`: `Game` class, main loop, input handling, state orchestration; `save_data` is every save the game makes (the periodic one on `World.AUTOSAVE_INTERVAL_S`, plus a tunnel entered or left, a night slept through, a quest handed in through `QuestSystem.on_complete`, a death and the way out) and stamps `last_save_ms`, which the HUD's corner marker counts down from; `_handle_key` is the whole in-world key map as one table (after the two ranges that read a key rather than name it: the weapon bar's number keys and the potion quickbar's letters), so a rebinding is one line there and one in `HelpMenu.CONTROLS`; `_pop_levelups` drains `player.stats.pending_levelups` each frame and pops the gold floating text/particle burst/chime over the player; `_respawn` handles death inside the run (penalty, heal, back to world spawn, brief death screen) so dying never bounces the player to the main menu, and `run()` is the loop alone (input, then `_update_frame` unless a menu is open, then `_draw_frame`, then the autosave check and the death check), closing the world and the name generator on the way out so their background threads stop writing to the save; `self.menus` is every full-screen menu, asked as one whether anything is open and therefore whether the world is paused; `_sweep_loot` is the whole of picking things up: anything lying within `Player.MAGNET_RADIUS` accelerates at the player and is collected on contact (a purse is credited and gone, a lootbox opens, everything else lands in the bag), skipping anything still hopping out of what dropped it and anything standing on another building's floor, so nothing is dragged through a wall; `current_interaction` returns the single `Interaction` E acts on right now (nearest of: a merchant/villager to talk to, a campfire to rest at, a shrine to pray at, a door to open or shut, an interior chest/bed, a village well to look down, the ladder back up out of a tunnel; loot is deliberately not on that list any more, the magnet owns it), which `_interact` dispatches and the renderer draws as the one on-screen prompt, so the prompt and the key can never disagree and a room full of beds can't stack labels; `_talk_to` refuses (and the prompt reads "<name> is busy...") while `llm_busy()` reports work in flight, so a conversation is never opened behind a call that has to finish first; `_use_door` opens or shuts a building's front door (and steps the player clear so shutting one can never wall them into the doorframe), the only way to put something between them and whatever is chasing them; `_threat_nearby` is the other refusal beside `llm_busy`, shared by the prompt and the key: nobody makes conversation with a monster within `Entities.TALK_SAFE_RADIUS`, and `_watched_label` names whoever can see the player over a chest or a bed; B (`_trade_nearby`) opens a nearby merchant's shop without going through the conversation (both it and `_talk_to` refuse anyone who has turned hostile); `_sleep_in_bed` is the one full rest in the game, healing everything and clearing the death weakness, unlike a campfire. No bed is bought and none of them is the player's: a tavern room is taken exactly as a villager's bed is, so both cost the risk of being seen (`_check_witness`) and both leave that bed cold for `REST_COOLDOWN_S`. Nobody lies down with something hostile within `Buildings.SLEEP_SAFE_RADIUS` (`_sleep_threat`, read by the prompt and the key alike, the same refusal `camp_is_clear` makes at a fire), and the night is not skipped instantly but by `_sleep_until_dawn`, which fades the screen out and back while `World.pass_time` runs the sky and every clock in the world forward to just after dawn and the player's potion buffs wear off; the same check `_open_interior_chest` runs after emptying somebody's chest (whoever saw it turns on the player alone, through `World.catch_thief`); `_respawn` also pops a taunt off `DeathTauntGenerator` and reads `Player.last_hit_by`, which is what the death screen mocks the player with; the world's chunks are streamed in `__init__` through `World.prepare`, so the ground exists before the first frame the player controls rather than being generated under them; both the initial spawn and `_respawn` place the player through `World.safe_spot_near` so neither starts inside a wall or under whatever killed them (`_respawn` first calls `World.clear_hostiles_around` on the spawn point), and both open the untouchable window with `Player.grant_spawn_grace` (`run()` rather than `__init__` on the way in, so it covers the first seconds of play rather than the model loading; after `run_game_over` rather than before, since the screen holds for seconds of wall-clock time), and `_announce_pickup` toasts every item that reaches the inventory (the F equip prompt when it beats its slot, its name otherwise, since a bow picked up under a stronger staff used to arrive silently)
- `src/game/world.py`: `World` class, the world state everything else reads: the entity lists (NPCs, monsters, bosses, items, POIs, critters), the per-frame `update`, building lookups, bosses, shops and saving. Four jobs live in their own modules and are mixed in (`class World(WorldCombat, WorldProjectiles, WorldStreaming, WorldPlaces)`), so look in `combat.py` for anything about a blow landing, in `projectiles.py` for anything in flight, in `streaming.py` for anything about the map coming into being, and in `places.py` for anything the player does at a place once they have walked to it. `__init__` is four steps and nothing else (`_init_state` declares every list and timer, `_load_persisted_state` reads what the save owns outright, then `_restore_saved_world` or `_create_new_world`), and `update` is the same shape (`_update_monsters`, `update_projectiles`, `_update_npcs`, `_update_critters`, `snap_traps`, then `_restock_surface`, which is the only part a tunnel skips). Here: `self.traps` are the loaded chunks' bear traps (with `trap_state`, which of them have shut) and `self.underground` is the tunnel the player is standing in, or None on the surface: it is the one switch that makes a tunnel somewhere else, since `blocked` answers with the rock instead of the map and `update` stops streaming chunks, revealing map cells, running events and standing anything up around the player while it is set (`tunnels` caches the ones built, `tunnel_state` is what each has left, `surface_return` is the well to come back out at); `persist_world` flushes generated state (context, shops, boss/landmark/village names) to disk when a background thread finishes it, throttled to `World.PERSIST_MIN_INTERVAL_S` (each call serialises the whole world) and skipped once `close()` has ended the session (a thread outliving its game must not write over the next one's save, and `close()` is also what silences `EventSystem`); `start_shop_generation` stocks every merchant still waiting in one batched call and `_shops_generating`/`_landmark_naming`/`_naming_villages` stop a second identical request being queued while the first is in flight, `sync_quest_boss_names` copies a boss's display name onto the slay_boss quest hunting it (the quest links by internal tag, which is never shown); `_restock_merchants` puts a locally rolled delivery on the shelf of any merchant whose clock has run out (never an LLM call: batching the generation is what made shops affordable); `_index_buildings`/`buildings_near`/`buildings_in_range` bucket buildings (and the solid parts of a village: its well, and a walled town's palisade and towers, `_village_solids_by_chunk`, read back by `walls_near` for the chase detour, which grows each palisade stretch to swallow the corner tower it ends at, since a tower left out of that was a third solid navigation could not see) by chunk so collision, drawing and swing tests stay flat as the world keeps gaining villages; `_update_npcs` is every villager's frame (fighting an intruder, running for a door, hunting the player, or wandering) and `_monster_target`/`_land_monster_blow` are the other half of the same fight: who a monster standing in a settlement comes for, and where its swing's damage is taken off; `assign_surround_slots(chasers, target)` is what makes a crowd an ambush rather than a queue, shared by monsters and by an angry village's mob: the chasers coming for one target are dealt evenly spaced bearings round it in the order they already stand in (each keeping the one it holds until the dealt one drifts `Entities.SLOT_REASSIGN_DEG` off it, so the ring settles instead of rotating every frame) and only `Entities.MAX_ACTIVE_ATTACKERS` of them, nearest first, may swing at any moment; `_mob_orders`/`_throw_stones` are what an angry village actually does about the player, the same split `militia_orders` makes about a monster: the militia close to arm's length and swing, everybody else holds `Villages.MOB_STANDOFF` back and throws stones (a hostile `Projectile` like any other, so it hits whatever is in the way), and anyone cut to `Villages.ROUT_HP_FRAC` of their health (`NPC.routed`) drops out of the fight and is sent to a door instead, so a mob breaks rather than dying to the last farmer; `chase_waypoint(chaser, player, radius)` is the navigation shared by monsters, bosses and angry villagers: buildings are the only obstacles and each has one door, so a chase across a wall aims for `_door_goal` (line up with the doorway, then step across the threshold, in or out, or come right up against it when the door is shut and there is nothing to do but beat on it), all of it written along the door's own outward normal so a house may face any of the four ways and `_detour_corner` walks round anything in the way by costing both ways round the rect (its obstacles are the buildings plus `_scenery_obstacles`, the solid trunks and boulders standing on the straight line over a short stretch, merged by `_merge_rects` so a copse is one thing to walk round rather than a dozen, since routing round each trunk in turn is how a monster picks its way into the middle of a wood) and heading for the first corner of the shorter one, over the buildings around the chaser by default and over the room's own furniture when both are standing in the same one (a corner it cannot stand in is dropped rather than walked at); `open_door_for` is a chasing villager letting themselves into their own street's house, the courtesy a monster is not given; `water_at(x, y)`/`terrain_speed(x, y)` are the other half of the terrain: water blocks nothing and slows everything (a bridge over it takes the point back to ordinary ground), the player through `Player.move`'s `in_water` and their swimming stat, monsters, bosses, critters and villagers through the `terrain_mult` their own update takes and never get better at; `line_of_sight(x0, y0, x1, y1)` walks a segment through the same `blocked` everything else uses, which is what stops a ranged monster firing at a player it cannot see, `night_damage_mult` is how much harder everything hits after dark, `quest_target` resolves where the tracked quest points (the only thing left with an offscreen arrow), `npc_in_reach` is the shared reach test behind the interaction prompt and the E/B keys, `_spawn_critter_away_from`/`critter_respawn_timer` keep wildlife populated around the player like monsters but with no combat cap (the species is rolled from the distance to the world center and pack/herd kinds are stood up as a group), `_post_guards` stands somebody at every gate, tower and (from tier 1) wall stretch of a walled town (an ordinary villager who always takes up arms, carries a real weapon and holds their post; how many of them and what they hold is the village's `tier`, and the ones in the towers and on the walls carry bows), `_set_toughness` is the one place a villager's health is set (their settlement's tier, and whether they are the militia or a guard: numbers, health and the wall are what a village is made strong with, never what its people are holding), `_bar_gates` shuts a settlement's gates while it is angry at the player and `_loose_arrows` is the posted archers shooting whatever their village is fighting, over their own wall (`blocked_over_walls`, and `line_of_sight(..., over_walls=True)`: a palisade beneath an archer neither hides the target nor stops the arrow, a house still does both) and crediting nobody, `_ensure_village_dogs` tops each nearby settlement back up to its own fixed number of dogs and `aggro_pack` is what turns an animal's kind (or a village's dogs) on the player together when one of them is struck, `blocked` also answers for the wilderness through `scenery_near` (the trunks and boulders bucketed on `Scenery.INDEX_CELL`) and `scenery_ground_in_range`/`scenery_props_in_range` are what the renderer walks (bucketed by chunk, ground kind by kind in draw order), `_signpost_directions` is a signpost reading out the nearest unexplored lead and marking it like a rumour, `unstick(body, radius)` is the one answer to a body having ended up *inside* something solid (every mover tests `blocked` at the point it wants to step to, so one already in a wall has every step refused and stays there for good): it is run once a frame over the villagers, the monsters and the wildlife, and over the player as a chunk streams in, and its search is deliberately short (`World.UNSTICK_RINGS`) so a body steps out of what it is in rather than being moved through it, `free_spot_near` returns the nearest standable point to a coordinate (the fixed spawn point sits inside the starting town's grid about four times in ten, so the player, not the settlement, is what moves) while `safe_spot_near` is the version used to place the *player*, rejecting any point with something hostile within `World.SAFE_SPOT_CLEARANCE` (`hostiles_near`) as well as any point in a wall, since a spot with no wall in it is not safe if what killed the player is standing on it, and `clear_hostiles_around` sends the roaming pack that followed them there back out into the wilds on a respawn (bosses and camp garrisons stay: dying is not a way to be rid of them); `roaming_cap(player)` is how many roaming monsters the world holds right now, ramped by the player's distance from the world centre (`World.ROAMING_CAP_NEAR` on the starting town's doorstep to `ROAMING_CAP_FAR` past `ROAMING_CAP_FAR_DISTANCE`, eased by `ROAMING_CAP_CURVE` so the first walk out stays quiet and the crowding arrives at the same depth the tougher kinds do) rather than one world-wide number, and a new world is created holding the *near* cap, since everything placed at creation lands within despawn range of the spawn point, and `_spawn_is_sheltered` is the ground nothing hostile is ever spawned on: inside `World.SAFE_RADIUS` of the world centre, or on any settlement's grounds plus `VILLAGE_SPAWN_MARGIN` (`village_at` takes that margin), so standing in town produces no respawns at all and the player has to walk out to find the fight
- `src/game/combat.py`: `WorldCombat`, mixed into `World`: every blow and its aftermath. `handle_attack` is the one entry point for a swing (left click) or a shot (right click): it resolves the weapon's archetype into reach/damage/cadence/cleave, draws the arc, then hands the blow to `_swing_at_bodies` and, only if that hit nothing, to `_swing_at_scenery` (crates, caches, props, windows). `_swing_at_bodies` walks one table of target groups in priority order (bosses, monsters, whatever is already fighting back, peaceful wildlife, peaceful villagers), stopping at the first with anything in reach; each row carries a `hostile` flag, and that flag alone is what a cleave carries on into, so reordering the table can never quietly widen a sweep onto bystanders; `_cleave_falloff` is what a cleaving swing is worth per head, full for whatever the weapon is pointed at and down to `Combat.CLEAVE_MIN` at the edge of the arc or the end of the reach, so a wide weapon is worth swinging into a crowd and still worth aiming, and a cleave carries on through the hostile groups under the one it engaged (a sweep catching a goblin and the villager swinging beside it hits both) while never reaching the peaceful ones; `_targets_in_reach` takes the attacker's own position, the weapon's `min_hit_distance` so a spear can't hit what is pressed against the player, and the wedge (`WeaponArchetype.arc_deg`, tested by `_within_arc`) the swing covers, or, for a `pierce_melee` weapon, swaps that wedge for a lane (`_targets_in_lane`, a fixed width down the facing rather than a widening cone, with `_thrust_shares` giving the first body on the shaft the whole blow and each one behind it `Combat.THRUST_FALLOFF` of the one before); whichever of the two it is, it is exactly what `core.swing_arcs` draws on screen, so a cleaving weapon visibly sweeps what it is about to hit, a thrust visibly covers its lane, and neither catches anything behind the player; `_strike_*`/`_resolve_*_hit` apply damage, knockback (skipped for `knockback_immune` bosses), crit and death; `_strike_critter`/`_kill_critter` make wildlife huntable (an animal already biting is struck right after monsters, the peaceful ones after that and before villagers, so a swing meant for a wolf never lands on a fox and hunting in town starts no brawl) and every hit on one goes through `World.aggro_pack`, so wounding one pack animal answers with the pack; `_spill_blood` is the shared gore of any death (pool, directional droplet fan, blood and body-coloured particles), taking the killing blow's direction so the mess points away from the player; `_apply_on_hit_effects`/`_apply_chainstrike` are the weapon affixes that fire after a hit lands (Chain Strike is an area effect: everything within `Affixes.CHAINSTRIKE_RADIUS` of what was struck takes a share, and the ring it damaged over plus a bolt to each thing it caught is drawn through `core/impact_fx.py`, so several numbers popping at once have something visible behind them), and `_apply_element`/`_chain_bolt` are what an elemental staff's bolt does where it landed: fire lights the burn ticker, frost chills whatever it touched, storm jumps to the nearest other body; `Building.damage_prop_at`/`_hit_poi`/`_hit_breakable`/`_hit_window` are the smashable world, and nothing in it breaks in one tap: each takes the swing's damage off a hit-point pool (`Breakables.HP`, `Buildings.FURNITURE_HP`/`WINDOW_HP`, `PointsOfInterest.CACHE_HP`), spends a `_prop_chip` puff while it holds (which also stamps `core.damage_fx` so the prop itself flinches and cracks), and only calls the matching `_break_*` (loot, debris, removal) on the blow that finishes it; `_gate_in_reach`/`_hit_gate`/`bash_gates` are the one part of a town's wall that answers a swing, the player hacking their way out of a settlement that has barred its gates on them and a pack beating its way in, resolved on a hit-point pool exactly like a front door; `prick_spikes`/`_spike_victim` are the stakes outside a wall biting whoever walked into them, on their own cooldown and crediting nobody, the bear trap's rule; `snap_traps`/`_spring_trap` are a bear trap shutting on whoever stood on it first, player or monster or animal or villager, resolved `by_player=False` since the player did not set it: the health it takes is small and the root is the point; `explode` is one blast wherever it comes from, the one way to kill a crowd without a swing (damage falling off to the rim, the player included, chaining into any keg nearby, kills credited to whoever set it off): its radius/damage/knockback/credit are keyword arguments, so a powder keg is the defaults and `detonate_creeper` is the same call with `Creeper`'s numbers and `by_player=False`, taking the creeper off the map first so it is never caught in its own blast; `_resolve_npc_hit` is also what calls `World.provoke_village` (and `World.hold_grudge` on a death, the one anger no clock runs out on), so every path that lands on a villager (swing, arrow, cleave, blast) turns the settlement hostile the same way, and `_drop_villager_loot` is what a killed villager leaves: a `"coins"` purse item and sometimes a possession, both dropped on the ground rather than credited (`loot.loot_villager`, a merchant's worth more), so an uncredited kill still leaves them lying there for whoever walks over; everything about a shot, from loosing it to what it strikes, now lives in `projectiles.py`; wildlife is hit on `Critter.hit_radius`, not on `size`, in both the swing and the projectile test
- `src/game/projectiles.py`: `WorldProjectiles`, mixed into `World` beside `WorldCombat`: everything that travels. `_fire_ranged` puts the archetype's own `projectile_style` in the air (an arrow, a bolt coloured by the staff's element, or a boomerang, of which only one is ever in flight and which costs nothing to throw) and spends `Player.ready_ammo`, the quiver in the ammo slot or the cheapest one carried once that is spent, so choosing arrows never leaves a stocked player unable to shoot and the HUD's count is the stack actually being fired; `fire_monster_shots` is the ranged monsters' turn, spawning a hostile `Projectile` per archer/hexer in range on its own cooldown and only when `World.line_of_sight` says it can see the player and `Monster.cornered` says the player is not already on top of it, so breaking sight behind a wall and closing the gap are both real answers; `update_projectiles` walks the in-flight arrows/bolts and delegates each to `_projectile_hits_monster` (used for both `monsters` and `bosses`, which take a hit identically) then `_projectile_hits_critter`/`_projectile_hits_npc`/`_projectile_hits_keg`, all of them finding what they struck through the one `_projectile_target`, and `_projectile_after_hit` is what a pierce or a boomerang does instead of stopping. An arrow is not choosy about whose it is, so a monster's shot that misses the player hits whatever is standing behind them, and every method here carries a `by_player` flag: False means no xp, no lootbox, no quest counter, no provoked village and no pack aggro, since friendly fire kills but does not pay. Split off `combat.py` for the reason `combat.py` was split off `world.py`: an arrow has a lifetime of its own, where a swing resolves and is over
- `src/game/places.py`: `WorldPlaces`, mixed into `World`: what the player can do at a place once they have walked up to it, where `streaming.py` decides where that place is and when it exists. `_populate_camp` stands a bandit camp's surviving garrison up around its fire every time the chunk loads, rolling the size once on first sight and reading it from the camp's own `guards_alive`/`leader_alive` afterwards (each guard tagged with the camp's `camp_id`, which keeps it out of the roaming cap and out of the save), `on_guard_killed` strikes a fallen bandit off that count, which is the only thing that ever lowers it; a traveller camp instead gets its camper (once per save, `npc_spawned`). `camp_is_clear` gates both looting a bandit cache and resting: the garrison has to be `guards_defeated` *and* nothing hostile may be within `CAMP_CLEAR_RADIUS` right now, `camp_in_reach`/`rest_at_camp`/`rest_ready_in` are the fire the player rests at (a fraction of max HP back and the death debuff cleared, then that fire goes cold for `REST_COOLDOWN_S`, kept in `rest_cooldowns` and persisted so quitting can't reset it; that dict is keyed by POI id for a fire and by building id for a villager's bed, which `rest_in_house` puts on the same cooldown), `find_bandit_camp` looks for a camp still held for a clear_camp quest to name (loaded POIs first, then rings of chunks generated from their coordinates, so it can name one nobody has walked to) and `house_to_rob` picks the house a steal quest sends the player into (never one whose furniture had to be shuffled round the neck of an L until there was nowhere left to stand a chest), `provoke_village` turns a whole settlement hostile when one of its people is struck (affinity floored, quests dropped by the caller, dogs included, on a countdown that `hold_grudge` makes permanent once somebody dies), `militia_orders`/`_refuge_for` are what a settlement does about a monster in its street (who fights, who hides and where) while `theft_witness`/`can_see`/`catch_thief`/`report_crime` are the smaller version of that for stealing and for wrecking somebody's furniture: who can see the player from where they are standing right now (halved after dark, `Crime.WITNESS_RADIUS`, and now stopped by anything solid in between through `sight_reach`/`_sight_solid`, which sees through only the building the player is standing in, since a strict test would make every theft free), `vision_polygon` being that same walk turned into the wedge the renderer draws (cached per villager and recast only once they have moved `Crime.CONE_CACHE_MOVE` or turned `CONE_CACHE_TURN_DEG`, over solids gathered once by `sight_obstacles` rather than looked up per sample: a street of cones recast every frame cost more than the world behind them), and that one villager alone turning on them, `unexplored_lead` is the nearest village site or POI the player hasn't walked to yet (asked chunk by chunk, each one seeing only the buildings around it), behind both `_traveller_directions` (a camper pointing the way) and the rumours the minimap marks (`mark_rumor`/`_clear_reached_rumors`, session-only and wiped once the player gets there), `_signpost_directions` is a signpost reading out that same lead and marking it like a rumour, `pass_time(seconds)` runs the world forward without anything in it taking a step (the day cycle, plus every wall-clock deadline that would otherwise sit untouched through a skipped night: rest cooldowns and village anger, never a grudge), which is what sleeping calls, `shrine_in_reach`/`pray_at_shrine` are the one prayer a shrine answers (persisted `prayed`, mostly a timed potion buff, `SHRINE_CURSE_CHANCE` of the time a curse: the Weakened state, a bite out of the purse or out of the health bar), `well_in_reach`/`tunnel_for`/`enter_tunnel`/`leave_tunnel`/`abandon_tunnel` are the well and what is under it (every settlement's well can be looked down, only some go anywhere; climbing down is a change of position and of what `World.update` bothers doing, not a load), `_populate_tunnel` stands its garrison back up from a count the way `_populate_camp` does (tagged with the tunnel's id as `camp_id`, so those monsters never despawn, never count toward the roaming cap and are never saved) and puts its hoard out once ever, `on_guard_killed` answers for that count as well as a camp's, and `_restore_underground` is a game saved underground being loaded underground; `_reveal_around`/`is_explored` keep the explored-cell memory the minimap draws, and `_check_poi_discovery` fires a shrine saying what it is and what praying does, a quiet landmark saying what it is, or a camper's directions
- `src/game/streaming.py`: `WorldStreaming`, mixed into `World`: how the endless map appears around the player and what it gets called. `_sync_chunks`/`_load_chunk`/`_unload_chunk` generate and drop a chunk's floor details, wilderness (`game/entities/scenery.py`) and landmarks from its coordinates alone (`World.poi_state` holds the only part a player can change), and `_sync_chunks` ends by stepping the player out of anything a freshly generated chunk stood on top of them; a chunk's bear traps are laid last of all (`traps_for_chunk`, after its wilderness exists, so none of them ends up under a trunk or in the water where nothing could step on it) and leave with it, carrying only whether they have shut; `_reindex_scenery` rebuilds the per-chunk draw buckets and the collision grid once per sync, `_clear_scenery_for` cuts back the trees a new settlement was built in (its chunk's neighbours may have grown them first), `_ensure_village` builds the settlement a chunk's `village_site` offers, once ever, and keeps it in the save; `prepare` builds the ring of chunks around the player before the game loop starts, so the streaming happens while the opening lore holds on black instead of freezing the first frame the player controls; `_generate_context` is the world lore streamed at first launch, `_start_village_naming`/`_start_landmark_naming` name what has been generated on background threads, `_check_village_discovery` announces a village once its name is ready
- `src/game/events.py`: `EventSystem`, random world events (merchant, treasure, blood night, rumours, village crisis); a travelling merchant is set down on a road where there is one (`_road_point_near`), walks it with a hired guard beside them (their wander anchor drifts, so the pair make their way across the map with no pathfinding of their own) and is only taken off the map once the player is far enough away not to watch them go; `blood_intensity` is the one number the blood night is read through, ramping up over `Events.BLOOD_NIGHT_FADE_MS` and back down at the far end, so the sky tint (`DayNightCycle.draw`), the respawn rate (`World.update`) and the loot odds (`WorldCombat`) all move together instead of the pressure arriving a frame before the colour. - `src/game/entities/monsters.py`: `Monster(Entity)`, `pick_monster_kind` (spawn selection by distance from center: a kind unlocks at its `min_distance` and then *fades out* again, its weight halving every `Entities.DEPTH_HALF_LIFE` past that point down to `DEPTH_MIN_WEIGHT_FRAC`, so the deep wilds are made of the deep kinds rather than of slimes with the occasional troll), `apply_burn` + burn-tick state (weapon burn affix, ticked in `World.update`), `knockback_immune` (False here, True on `Boss`) and `camp_id`, the bandit camp a guard was posted at: a tagged monster never despawns and never counts toward the roaming population cap, so a camp can only be emptied by killing it; `_steer` probes a lookahead distance rather than one step so a monster turns away from a wall early instead of grinding into it, never probing past the goal itself (a wall behind the player is not in the way) and falling back to a short probe when every long one is blocked, which is what gets it into a corner or between two pieces of furniture, with each probe sampled along its length rather than at the tip alone (a clear point beyond a thin wall is not a clear path to it), the side it deflected to held for `World.STEER_COMMIT_MS` and tried first next frame so it walks round an obstacle instead of re-deciding at the seam and shivering there, and a wall-follow at the end of it so a boxed-in monster runs along what is in the way rather than into it; `attack_token` is the world's permission to swing (`World.assign_surround_slots`) and one held back off the tokens circles its bearing round the ring instead of standing there, which is what a pack does rather than queueing; `_charge` is the Ogre's rush (plant, telegraph the locked-in lane with `_draw_charge_telegraph`, then cross the gap at `Charge.SPEED_MULT`, no special damage, the ordinary swing does that) and `_flank` is the Wraith's refusal to walk straight in (approach bent to one side, flipping every few seconds, fading out as it closes so it still arrives); - `src/game/entities/monster_art.py`: the vector art behind every hostile creature, one function per silhouette (`humanoid`, `goblin`, `hulk`, `skeleton`, `wraith`, `blob`, `beast`, `robed`, `creeper`), picked by `MonsterKind.shape` the way `CritterKind.shape` picks an animal's. `draw_monster` is the one entry point: it lays down the ground shadow, calls the shape function (which draws in the creature's own unrotated space, forward at -y, and hands back where the eyes and hands ended up), puts whatever the kind carries in the returned hand through the same `gear.draw_weapon` the player's gear uses, finishes with the glowing eyes, and rocks and lifts the whole body by how far through its stride it is (`walk`, off the shared `Gait`) while the shadow stays where it was laid. Everything above the silhouette is shared, because a creature is read from three things before its outline: the shadow saying it stands on the ground, the breath saying it is alive, and the eyes saying it has seen you. Kept apart from `monsters.py` for the reason `item_icons.py` is kept apart from `items.py`
- `src/game/entities/boss.py`: `Boss(Monster)`, a named LLM-titled boss with an enrage phase and telegraphed abilities (slam AoE, hostile bolt volley, summon adds), knockback immune; spawned at the landmark, by roaming, by events and by slay_boss quests. `_kind_from_boss` copies the template's `shape`/`weapon`/`eye_color` onto the synthesised `MonsterKind`, so a boss is drawn by `monster_art` like anything else (a brute is a `hulk` with a hammer, a warlock a `robed` figure with a staff) with its aura, slam telegraph and name label layered on, its eyes always lit and brightened once enraged
- `src/game/entities/village.py`: `Village`, `village_site`, `sites_near_chunk`, `generate_village`, `generate_starting_world`. Settlements are how buildings exist: a square region of chunks settles at most one of them (`_region_site`, a pure function of the coordinates, with a later region standing down when its site lands within `MIN_GAP` of an earlier one), and the cluster is laid out on a grid around an open plaza whose middle slot is left free for the well `Village.draw`/`Village.blocks` own. Unlike a POI a village is generated once and then saved (its NPCs carry names, affinity, quests and stock, none of which a chunk seed can rebuild): `village_site` says where, `World._ensure_village` builds it, the save keeps it. `village_site`/`_region_site` are cached (pure functions of their coordinates, asked over and over by chunk loading, POI placement and the roads), and `sites_near_chunk` is the list of sites around a chunk the roads are drawn between. `generate_starting_world` is the town the player opens in plus the ruined landmark placed alone in the settled ring. `_plaza_slots` keeps the middle of that grid open as a keep-out rather than by dropping a slot from a list (a grid with an even number of columns has no centre slot, which is how a house came to be built on the well), and `_clear_of_plaza`/`_separate` then step a building off the square and out of its neighbour, since one with a wing on it is wider than the slot it was dealt. A settlement big enough to be worth defending (`Villages.WALLED_SIZES`, plus the starting town) stands a wall: `defences()` builds a ring set `WALL_MARGIN` outside the last house (off `extent_x`/`extent_y`, the real footprint per axis, so a settlement that spread sideways is walled in a rectangle following it rather than in a square around its longest side), split by a gate in the middle of each side, thickened into a gatehouse either side of every gateway, closed by a tower at each corner and, from tier 1, backed by a row of stakes and from tier 2 by a ditch (`spike_hit`/`in_ditch`, one costing health and the other speed), all of it solid through `blocks` and drawn by `_draw_defences`; `tier` (`_tier_for`, rolled once from the distance to the world centre and the size, then persisted) is the one number behind how well defended a place is: the wall's material and thickness, its towers' size, how many stand on it, whether any of them carry a bow, whether there are outworks, and which weapon ladder its people draw from. The gates are the one part of a wall that gives: they stand open until `World._bar_gates` shuts them because the settlement has turned on the player (`barred`), and a barred one is a hit-point pool exactly like a front door (`gate_closed`/`gate_at`/`damage_gate`, `gate_broken` persisted, `gate_hp` session-only). `grounds_radius` is how far the settlement's grounds then reach, out to the corner towers on the diagonal rather than to the nearest stretch of wall, which is what decides who turns on the player, who defends the place, where nothing hostile may be stood up and how far the trees are cut back; `site_grounds_radius(cx, cy)` is that same answer for a village nobody has generated yet, a pure function of the coordinates (the size is the first draw `generate_village` makes off the same seed, at its largest possible footprint), which is what a landmark clears instead of clearing the site point by a fixed distance
- `src/game/entities/buildings.py`: `Building`, `set_active_buildings`, one building's footprint, interior and drawing (placement belongs to `village.py`). A building is one rect or two: `footprint()` is every rect it is built of, `bounds` the box they all fit in (what the chunk index, a chase detour and the tree and bear-trap clearances ask for), and `wing()` is the second one that makes it an L, rolled from the building's own id like its roof so it needs no field in the save. A wing always grows out of the back half of one side, which is what keeps the facade one straight wall: the door, the windows, the awning and the doorstep know nothing about it. `_wing_opening` is the one rect that both cuts the wall between the two halves out of the shell (`_subtract`) and joins their floors into the one connected space `interior_rects` returns and `contains_point`/`blocks` walk, turned out of the canonical room like everything else so what a body walks on and what the furniture was laid out over can never disagree. A building has a `facing`: which of the four walls holds the front door, set by the village layout so a door opens onto the plaza and persisted, since the same house in another slot would face another way. Everything about the facade is written as an offset along `outward()` through the one `_facade_slot`/`_facade_band` pair (the door, its trigger zone, the windows, the doorstep, an awning, a porch, the tavern's sign), and the interior is laid out once in a canonical room whose door is in the bottom wall and then turned onto the real one by `_place`, so every arrangement can go on saying "against the back wall" and mean it; that room is a `_RoomSpace` (the floor, the corridor kept clear from the door, what has been put down so far, and `try_place`/`add_crates` for putting more down), handed to one `_lay_out_house`/`_lay_out_shop`/`_lay_out_tavern` per kind; its `keep_clear` list is every way through the room furniture may not stand in (the corridor in from the door, the neck of an L), which `add` nudges a fixed piece out of and which `try_place` rolls around, over every piece of floor by area, so a wing is furnished rather than left empty. A building's interior is its own footprint in world space (`interior_rect`), not a separate room: `blocks` collides against a thin wall shell (`_wall_segments`) whose door gap is filled by the door itself while it is shut plus furniture (`interior_layout`, deterministic per building id, whose `beds` list holds both a tavern's row of them and a house's single one, since the player can sleep in either), and `contains_point`/`draw(..., player_inside=True)` swap that one building to a roofless cutaway view when the player is standing in it, drawn by `_draw_interior`. Every stick of furniture in a room comes apart (`damage_prop_at` takes a swing's damage off that piece's own pool and reports `(index, rect, kind, destroyed)`, hit points per kind in `Buildings.FURNITURE_HP`, the pool session-only in `prop_hp` and only the finished ones saved in `broken_props`, wreckage drawn by `_draw_debris`), a bed and a chest deliberately excepted since both are mechanics rather than props; the two kinds that hold wares (`Buildings.FURNITURE_LOOT`) drop an item into per-building `dropped_items` (not persisted), picked up through `Game.current_interaction` (the interior draws the loot but no prompts, so only one prompt is ever on screen); `window_rects` gives the two windows flanking the door on every non-landmark building's facade, shatterable (`broken_windows` state, persisted). The front door is its own thing: shut until somebody opens it (`door_closed`, `toggle_door` off the player's E), a wall while it is, stepped out of rather than shut on somebody (`door_overlaps`/`clear_of_door`: one step in or out along the wall's own normal, and E on a door the player is standing in only ever opens it), beaten down over several blows by a monster that cannot reach the player any other way (`damage_door`, session-only `door_hp` like a crate's, persisted `door_open`/`door_broken`), and drawn in whichever of the three states it is in by `_draw_door`, cracks and all, from the same rect the collision shell uses. `style()` is how a street stops being the same house repeated: rolled once from the building's id (so it survives a save) into a roof material and form (`_draw_roof`/`_draw_roof_texture`: thatch, tile, shingle or slate over a gable, hip or flat roof), a wall tint and one or two extras drawn by `_draw_extras` (a smoking chimney, a porch, shutters, a flower box, a woodpile). The kind still reads first: a shop keeps its awning and a tavern its sign whatever it is roofed with
- `src/game/entities/scenery.py`: `Scenery`, `generate_chunk_scenery`, `road_points_for_chunk`, `river_points_for_chunk`, `blocking_index`, `water_index`: the wilderness between the villages. A chunk rolls one biome (`Scenery.BIOME_WEIGHTS`) and lays down what that biome is made of in clumps, so a wood reads as a wood rather than as an even sprinkle: ground patches, ponds, grass, flowers, pebbles under the entities, trees/pines/boulders/stumps/reeds over them. `_DRAWERS` names what draws each kind, explicitly rather than off the kind's own name, so a search for `_draw_boulder` finds both ends of it. Trunks and boulders are the only things that stop movement (`blocks`, reached through `World.blocked` via the fine grid `blocking_index` builds), and roads join each village site to its nearest neighbour, bent by a seeded sine and kept clear of anything solid. Water is the other half of that: `river_points_for_chunk` runs a river along every lane (`Scenery.RIVER_LANE_CHUNKS`, a pure function of the lane's own index, so a chunk lays down its stretch and the course still joins up across the seam), bent around any settlement it would otherwise run through (`_dodge_villages`) and carrying a bridge at fixed intervals plus one wherever a road meets it (`_road_crossings`), while ponds and lakes are the same water standing still. Nothing about water blocks: `covers`/`water_reach` feed the `water_index` behind `World.water_at`, and what it costs to cross is a speed penalty, not a wall. Water is drawn in layers rather than per blob (a blob painting its own bank, body and middle paints over its neighbour's, which read as scales): a river blob draws only its bank and the `river_body`/`river_deep` kinds standing at the same points are laid down in their own `Scenery.GROUND_KINDS` passes, while a pond or lake holds its own clutch of lobes and draws all of them bank-first. `_chunk_terrain` is the chunk's roads, river and crossings rolled once and cached, since a chunk asks its eight neighbours for their bridges too: nothing solid may stand at the end of a deck, and a deck laid near a seam is walked onto from next door. Streamed and dropped with its chunk exactly like the floor details, never saved, so nothing the player does can change it
- `src/game/entities/traps.py`: `BearTrap`, `traps_for_chunk`, the hunters' bear traps set in the woods around a settlement. Generated per chunk from its coordinates like scenery (only in the band `Traps.MIN_FROM_VILLAGE`..`MAX_FROM_VILLAGE` around a village site: out in the deep wilds nobody would come back to check the line), but unlike scenery a trap has one piece of state worth keeping, so `World.trap_state` persists which ones have already shut, keyed by trap id, exactly the way `poi_state` works. Nobody laid it for the player and nobody owns it: `catches` is tested against the player, monsters, wildlife and villagers alike in `WorldCombat.snap_traps`, and the first thing to stand on it springs it. What it costs is a bite of health and, mostly, `Traps.HOLD_MS` of being rooted where you stand
- `src/game/entities/tunnel.py`: `Tunnel`, `has_tunnel`, the dug-out under a village well. Not a separate scene: a tunnel is a handful of rooms and corridors carved out of ordinary world space a very long way from any ground that ever streams in (`Tunnels.ORIGIN`, one grid slot per village chunk), which is what lets movement, collision, combat, loot and the save work down there without any of them knowing where they are. Collision is the *floor* rather than the walls (`blocks` asks whether the body's footprint, tested at its centre and corners, lies on the *union* of the floor rectangles rather than inside any one of them, since a body straddling a doorway fits in neither; the legs of every corridor run centre to centre so the union stays connected), `at_exit` is the shaft back up, `floor_spots` is where anything stood up down there goes, `draw` lays the rock down and cuts the floor out of it in two passes (an outline would draw a wall across every doorway, a doorway here being where two rectangles overlap) and `draw_dark` is the player's own light, the tunnel's real difficulty. `has_tunnel` is a pure function of the village's chunk, so only some wells go anywhere and finding one that does is worth the walk over to look
- `src/game/entities/breakables.py`: `Breakable`, `generate_breakables`, outdoor props scattered near houses/shops/taverns (deterministic per building, weighted by kind): "barrel" gives the same coin/item odds as a shop crate; "powder" is a keg that pays nothing and detonates instead (`WorldCombat.explode`), the only prop an arrow can set off, drawn squat and iron-banded with a lit fuse so it is never mistaken for a barrel; "bush"/"flowerbed"/"herbs"/"sapling" are what is planted around a doorstep, pure decoration with no reward ("pot" is only drawn for saves made before the clay pots were replaced). Each carries persisted `hp` (`Breakables.HP`) so it takes several swings to clear, shows how far along it is as it goes (a hard prop cracks through `core.damage_fx`, a planted one loses bulk and blooms), and is gone for good once broken, no debris state (unlike interior crates)
- `src/game/entities/poi.py`: `PointOfInterest`, `pois_for_chunk`, wilderness landmarks generated per chunk from its coordinates (at most one, kept clear of the chunk's own edges, of buildings and of every village site nearby, generated or not) so the world is endless and walking out always finds something; a POI is fully described by its chunk seed apart from `state()`/`apply_state()` (looted, discovered, npc_spawned, guards_alive, leader_alive), which is all `World` saves, keyed by the POI's `"cx:cy"` id; "ruins" is a smashable loot cache (`looted` persisted, left in place afterwards, just visibly picked over) via `game.loot.open_poi_cache`, "shrine" says what it is and what praying does the first time it is walked up to (`discovered` persisted) and then answers one prayer, for good or ill (`prayed` persisted, no loot), and "camp" rolls a `variant` from its id: its cache takes several blows to force (`cache_hp`, session-only: a POI is rebuilt from its chunk seed anyway); a "bandit" camp is a garrison held as a count (`guards_alive`/`leader_alive`, `guard_killed`, `guards_defeated`) around a cache that only opens once they are dead (its tents are cut down and its banner drops when looted), a "traveller" camp is a camper who trades and gives directions. Both keep their fire lit whatever happens to them, because the fire is what the player comes back to rest at. Beside those three: "farmstead" is a second lootable cache (`has_loot`) with a barn and a fence of its own, "graveyard" is laid out on real rows (one stone size, a fixed spacing, a fence round the plot and only enough jitter to say the ground settled unevenly), and it, "watchtower" and "stones" are places rather than rewards, each saying what it is the first time it is reached (`PointsOfInterest.LANDMARK_MESSAGES`), and "signpost" reads out the way to somewhere never walked to and marks it on the minimap (`World._signpost_directions`)
- `src/game/entities/critter.py`: `Critter`, `pick_critter_kind` (species by distance from the world center, like a monster kind), every animal in the world. What one does with the player is entirely its `CritterKind.temperament`: "passive" (rabbit/deer/fox) only runs, "retaliate" (badger/boar) answers a blow and then breaks off at `BREAK_OFF_HP_FRAC`, "predator" (wild dog/bear) hunts on sight inside `detection`, "guard" (dog) is peaceful until its village or camp turns. `_should_hunt`/`_hunt` are the chase and the bite (contact damage on a cooldown, `lunge_until_ms` the only tell it came from the animal), `_fleeing`/`_flee` the running: a fleeing animal commits to a heading and bends it only `FLEE_TURN_RATE_DEG` a frame, sprints for its `stamina_ms` and then trots at `TIRED_MULT`, so catching one is a question of its wind rather than of out-turning a jitter. Huntable on a `hit_radius` scaled off its size rather than half of it, since none of them are drawn as a circle; killed in `WorldCombat._kill_critter` for a sellable pelt/tusk/hide. Session-only like particles or projectiles (never saved): wildlife is spawned/despawned around the player by `World`, in herds or packs (`CritterKind.group`), and dogs are stood back up from their village (`World._ensure_village_dogs`) or camp (`_populate_camp`) the way a garrison is. Drawn as a small ellipse-and-head or as a real quadruped (`_draw_quadruped`: flank polygon, legs cycled in diagonal pairs off its own `Gait`, neck, plus per-kind antlers/tusks/ears/tail) because a plain ellipse with two lines off the head reads as a snail from a top-down camera, with a small health bar once wounded
- `src/game/entities/items.py`: `Item` (weapon/armor/shield/accessory/ammo/potion/misc; ammo and potions stack via `quantity`, misc are sellable "valuables" drawn as a coin; `SHIELD_KEYWORDS` is checked before the armour ones in `item_type_from_name`, so anything named a shield/buckler/targe is its own offhand item type rather than body armour, and its `bonus` doubles as flat defence and as how much it blocks), potions (`potion_effect_from_name` reads heal/regen/strength/swiftness/stoneskin out of the name, `potion_magnitude`/`potion_duration` scale it by rarity, `potion_description` writes the tooltip line, drawn as a "flask" coloured by effect), rarity rolling (`roll_rarity`, which takes a `luck` that leans every step of the ladder up by that much and is the whole of what the luck accessory does, `rarity_tier`, `rarity_color`), `roll_bonus`, `roll_affixes`/`affix_label` (weapon/armor special effects stored in `Item.affixes`), expanded `ACCESSORY_FLAVORS` (+crit/lifesteal/coinfind/xpgain/pierce) with `ACCESSORY_FLAVOR_LABELS`, `base_value` (sell worth used by shop and inventory tooltip), `inventory_section`/`INVENTORY_SECTIONS` (which block of the bag an item reads under, melee and ranged being the same item type and told apart by archetype), `icon_shape` (the one place an item's icon is decided: a weapon takes its archetype's silhouette, dagger/sword/axe/hammer/spear/staff/bow, and body armour its own cuirass, so gear is told apart at a glance instead of every weapon being a generic sword and every plate a shield; the art itself lives in `item_icons.py`), the `"coins"` item type (a dropped purse holding its amount in `quantity`, credited on pickup and never carried), `magnet_toward` (the pull that collects loot, accelerating from `Player.MAGNET_SPEED_START` to `MAGNET_SPEED_MAX` and reporting when the item has reached the player), `start_pop_anim` for a dropped item hopping out of its source and settling, `_draw_ground_marker` (shadow plus a halo pulsing in the item's rarity colour, drawn only for loot lying in the world, never for HUD/menu icons) with a slow bob so loot is spotted from across the screen instead of blending into the grass
- `src/game/entities/item_icons.py`: `draw_shape_with_border`, the vector art behind every item icon: one function per shape `items.icon_shape` can return, gathered into `_SHAPES` at the bottom the way `monster_art.py` holds its silhouettes, each handed an `_Icon` with the frame it draws in (centre, size, colours, and `at(fx, fy)` for a point given as a fraction of the size) (weapon silhouettes, cuirass, shield, gem, arrow, flask, coin, chest). Kept apart from `items.py` so a search for what an item *is* never lands in a hundred lines of polygon coordinates; the HUD, the inventory, the shop and `gear.py` all draw through it
- `src/game/entities/projectile.py`: `Projectile` plus `ARROW_COLOR`/`BOLT_COLOR`, a fired arrow or magic bolt travelling in a straight line until it hits or runs out of range (`style`, including the `"stone"` an angry villager throws, `color`, `knockback`, `shake`, `max_range` so the player's bow outreaches a monster's shot rather than everything in flight sharing one number, `hostile` for monster/boss bolts that damage the player, `by_player` for whether a kill by it pays the player at all (a town archer's arrow kills like any other and credits nobody), `over_walls` for a shot loosed from a tower, which clears its own settlement's palisade and nothing else, `pierce`/`hit_ids` for the arrow-pierce accessory, `owner_id` seeded into `hit_ids` so a shot can never strike its own shooter). `update` advances in hops no longer than the projectile is wide: a whole frame at once let an arrow cross a 16px wall whenever the framerate dipped. The one thing that does not fly straight is the `"boomerang"`: at the end of its throw, at the first wall, or once it has carried through `Boomerang.PIERCE` bodies, `turn_back` sends it home to `owner` (forgetting what it already struck, so the return leg is a second pass) and it is caught on contact, walls and range no longer applying to something coming back to a hand
- `src/game/entities/stats.py`: `Stats` class, use-based character progression (xp, training, derived bonuses like attack/damage reduction/speed, plus `swim_multiplier`, the one stat with no effect on land: it buys back part of the water penalty everything else in the world is stuck with, and never all of it); `train` queues `pending_levelups` on every level gained, drained each frame by `Game._pop_levelups`

### llm
- `src/llm/llm_request_queue.py`: `LLMRequestQueue`, serialises all LLM calls onto a worker thread (`get_llm_tasks` is what the HUD spinner reads), served in priority order: the categories in `INTERACTIVE_CATEGORIES` (the dialogue the player is waiting on) go ahead of background work, so quest analysis queued as one conversation closes no longer holds up the next NPC's greeting; ties break on arrival. Use `generate_response_queued` / `generate_response_stream_queued` (both take `max_tokens`, the streaming one also takes extra `stop` strings). A streamed response is cut at its first line break, so the model carrying on past its reply never reaches the caller. `poll=True` is what the main thread must pass on a stream: it drains the chunk queue instead of waiting on it and yields `None` when there is nothing new, since a running call cannot be preempted and blocking there froze the game until it finished; `llm_busy()` says whether anything is running or queued at all, which is what stops the player opening a conversation on top of it
- `src/llm/dialogue_manager.py`: `DialogueManager`, manages NPC dialogue window (streaming, quest detection on close); `_draw_purse` puts the player's coins beside a merchant's Shop button, haggling being the one conversation where what's in the purse decides what to say next; replies are capped at `Hyperparameters.DIALOGUE_MAX_TOKENS` with `DIALOGUE_STOPS` ("Player:") so an NPC can't ramble or write the player's turn, and `_trim_to_sentence` cuts a capped reply back to its last finished sentence; the streams are polled (`poll=True`), so `update` returns on a `None` chunk and the game keeps drawing while the reply is written, and the input box takes nothing while a reply is still streaming; `END_RE` recognises the conversation-ending marker in the shapes the model actually writes ([END], bare uppercase END closing the reply, bolded, parenthesised) rather than only the literal "[END]"
- `src/llm/quest_system.py`: `QuestSystem`, analyses conversation for quests, creates items, handles completion/rewards; `carried_item` is what hands an item quest in: the exact object the quest spawned, or anything else in the bag by that name, since an NPC asked for a thing rather than for one instance of it; `analyze_conversation_for_quest` asks the model both whether the NPC offered a task and whether the player took it (`player_accepted`), since an offer the player turned down is not a quest; each type is built by its own `_build_*` method, reached through the `_QUEST_BUILDERS` table `create_quest_from_analysis` dispatches on (a type the model invents falls back to fetch, and any builder may return None, which is how a quest with no target left in the world is dropped rather than handed over unfinishable) and finished by its own hook, `on_monster_killed`/`on_boss_killed`/`on_npc_killed` for the older ones and `on_camp_cleared` (a garrison emptied), `on_theft` (the named house's chest opened, which is when the stolen item is created) and `on_delivery` (the player walks up to the person a parcel is addressed to; the reward is still collected from whoever gave the quest) for the new ones, with `_pick_recipient` choosing the furthest NPC away so a delivery is a journey; `extract_and_give_reward` pays the coins the NPC named in their parting line but clamps them into that quest type's band (`c.QUEST_COIN_BANDS`, since the model has no sense of the economy) and `_reward_item` is the gear handed over, the thing the NPC promised or a rolled piece for the types in `Quests.ALWAYS_ITEM_TYPES` that always pay in more than coins; `on_complete` is set by `Game` and saves the game the moment a quest is handed in
- `src/llm/merchant_system.py`: `generate_shop_inventories`, stocks every merchant in one LLM call (one call for the whole town, not one per shop, which used to be the queue's biggest cost), each shop given a role (blacksmith/alchemist/general trader) so they differ; asks for compact `shop|name|type|rarity|price` lines and falls back to `loot.roll_shop_stock` for any shop the model fails to fill instead of retrying
- `src/llm/name_generator.py`: `NPCNameGenerator`, background-thread generation of NPC names ahead of time; persists the ready buffer and used-name history so a continued game reuses them instead of regenerating, and `close()` stops a name still in flight from writing into the next game's save
- `src/llm/death_taunts.py`: `DeathTauntGenerator`, the mocking line the death screen shows, written ahead of need on a background thread and buffered in the save (`Death.TAUNT_BUFFER`), exactly like the name generator and for the same reason: the death screen is blocking, so `take()` never waits and falls back to `Death.FALLBACK_TAUNTS` on an empty buffer; a generation that comes back empty stops the chain rather than re-arming, since one bad reply used to mean a call every few seconds for the rest of the session. The taunts never name a killer, since they are written before anyone knows who it will be; `Game._respawn` puts `Player.last_hit_by` on its own line instead

### core
- `src/core/constants/`: all game constants, one flat namespace (`import core.constants as c`) split into six modules by subject, so a new constant is filed by what it tunes rather than appended to one long file: `ui.py` (screen size, `Minimap`, colours, `Fonts`, LLM `Hyperparameters`), `player.py` (`Player`, `Death`, `Stats`/`STAT_LABELS`, `Affinity`), `combat.py` (`Shield`, `Projectile`, `Combat`, `WeaponArchetype` + `weapon_archetype`, `Decals`), `bestiary.py` (`Entities`, monsters, `Charge`/`Flank`, bosses, critters, `Wildlife`), `world.py` (`World`, `Events`, `DayNight`, `Crime`, `Buildings`, `Breakables`, `Tunnels`, `Traps`, `Scenery`, `Villages`, `Fog`, `PointsOfInterest`) and `items.py` (`Rarity`, `LootBox`, `Quests`/`QUEST_COIN_BANDS`, `Potions`, `Affixes`). `Fonts` is the one name rebound at runtime (`__main__` swaps the class for a loaded instance), which works because everything reads it off the package. What is in them: LLM hyperparameters including the shorter `DIALOGUE_MAX_TOKENS` cap on NPC replies; `Shield` (block fraction per point of the shield's bonus, the frontal arc it covers, the guard meter and what breaking it costs); `Death` (coin loss, how long the post-death "Weakened" state cuts damage, speed and max HP, `SPAWN_GRACE_S` for how long nothing lands on a freshly spawned player, plus the death-screen taunt buffer and its canned fallbacks); `Crime` (how far a villager sees the player stealing, how much of that is left after dark, the `VIEW_CONE_DEG` wedge they see it in, and how long the one who catches them stays angry); `DayNight.NIGHT_*` (after dark: respawn rate, damage, detection range, the danger bonus new spawns roll with, and the roaming-boss multiplier); `Charge`/`Flank`, the rush and the circling approach behind `MonsterKind.charge`/`flank_deg`, `Creeper` (the fuse, the blast and the range that lights it, behind `MonsterKind.detonate`), and `World.PACK_SPREAD` for how far a pack kind is scattered on spawn; `Entities.SLOT_REASSIGN_DEG`/`MAX_ACTIVE_ATTACKERS`/`CIRCLE_SPEED_DEG` for how a crowd surrounds something and how few of it may swing at once, `Entities.GAIT_*` for the one walk cycle everything animates from, `World.STEER_COMMIT_MS`/`STEER_PROBE_SAMPLES`/`SCENERY_DETOUR_RANGE` for the navigation round a wood, `Villages.MOB_STANDOFF`/`MOB_STONE_*`/`ROUT_HP_FRAC` for what an angry village does about the player, `Combat.CLEAVE_MIN`/`CLEAVE_ANGLE_SHARE` for what a cleaving swing is worth away from where it was aimed and `Projectile.RANGE`/`MONSTER_RANGE` for how far a shot carries in each direction; `WeaponArchetype` per-family combat feel, including the `tool` a farmer fights with (reach/swing/damage/cooldown/knockback/crit/cleave/shake, plus `min_hit_distance`, the polearm blind spot that makes the spear hit a ring instead of a disc so nothing pressed against the player is hit, `arc_deg`, the wedge the swing both covers and draws, `pierce_melee`, the lane a thrust runs down instead of that wedge, and `projectile_style`/`element` for what a ranged one puts in the air) resolved by `weapon_archetype(name)`, which is also where an elemental staff is built as a variant of the one staff profile rather than as a family of its own, plus `Combat` tuning (including the swing-arc look), `Explosion` (a powder keg's radius, falloff, what it costs the player and how far it chains), `Combat.THRUST_*` (the width of a thrust's lane and what each body behind the first takes of it), `Staffs`/`STAFF_BOLT_COLORS`/`STAFF_ELEMENT_WORDS` (what a fire/frost/storm staff's bolt does beyond damage, and the name words that make a staff elemental), `Boomerang` (how far it is thrown, how many bodies one pass carries through and how fast it comes home), `ImpactFx` (the ring an area effect draws) and `DamageFx` (how long a struck prop flinches, how many cracks a nearly-broken one carries); `Affixes` weapon/armor effect pools + rarity-scaled magnitudes and burn timing; `Potions` per-effect rarity-scaled magnitudes, buff durations, liquid colours and quickbar size; `BossKind`/`BOSS_KINDS` archetype templates (brute/warlock/colossus) and `Boss` tuning (enrage, abilities, rewards, spawn caps, health bar); `Tunnels` what is under a village well (how many wells go anywhere, the far corner of world space the rooms are dug in, room and corridor sizes, the garrison and the hoard, and how far the player can see down there) and `Traps` the hunters' bear traps (the band around a settlement they are laid in, how many, what one takes off you and how long it holds you); `Scenery` the wilderness: what blocks and at what radius, the ground kinds and their draw order, the per-biome composition, ground-patch colours, clearances, the road tuning and the water (`WATER_KINDS`, river lanes/step/width/wobble and how far one bends round a village, pond and lake radii, bridge interval/size/colours, and `SWIM_SPEED`/`SWIM_SPEED_MAX`, what crossing costs everything in the world and what the player's swimming stat can buy back of it); `PointsOfInterest` per-chunk POI placement/loot tuning plus the shrine numbers (`SHRINE_MESSAGES`/`SHRINE_EXPLANATION`, `SHRINE_BLESSINGS`/`SHRINE_CURSES` and `SHRINE_CURSE_CHANCE`) plus the camp numbers (bandit-vs-traveller odds, guard count/spread/leader bonus, `CAMP_CLEAR_RADIUS`, `REST_DISTANCE`, camper stock and hint range); `Buildings.WING_*` (which kinds are built as an L, how big the second rect is and how clear its neck is kept); `Villages.TIER_*`/`WALL_STYLE_BY_TIER`/`GUARDS_PER_POST_BY_TIER`/`ARCHERS_PER_TOWER_BY_TIER`/`ARCHERS_PER_WALL_BY_TIER`/`GATE_HP`/`SPIKE_*`/`DITCH_*`/`HP_BY_TIER`, the whole of how much better defended a settlement is for standing further out; `Villages` settlement spacing (`REGION_CHUNKS`/`REGION_CHANCE`/`MIN_GAP`, deliberately wide: a village is meant to be a find, which only works because the wilderness between them has cover, landmarks and roads), grid slots, per-size composition and plaza/well look; `Villages.ANGER_S`/`ANGER_CAP_S` (how long a settlement stays furious and the ceiling repeat offences push that to) and `MILITIA_FRACTION`/`DEFEND_MARGIN`/`DEFEND_RADIUS`/`PANIC_RADIUS` (who takes up arms against a monster in the street, how far they go to meet it and how close one has to be before everybody else bolts); `Fog` explored-cell size and reveal radius, `Minimap` size/range/palette plus the rumour mark and the day/night clock strip drawn under it; `World.PERSIST_MIN_INTERVAL_S`, the floor on how often a background thread may serialise the whole world to disk, and `World.AUTOSAVE_INTERVAL_S`, how often the game saves itself while it is being played; `World.STEER_LOOKAHEAD`/`STEER_MIN_PROBE`/`STEER_CLOSE_PROBE`/`DOOR_APPROACH_DISTANCE` for monster navigation and `Entities.CHASE_RING_MARGIN`/`CHASE_ARRIVE`/`SEPARATION_PUSH`/`RETREAT_SPEED_MULT`/`CORNERED_FRAC`/`RANGED_MELEE_RANGE` for how a pack spreads round the player and how an archer behaves once caught, `Buildings.DOOR_HP`/`DOOR_BASH_REACH`/`DOOR_BASH_COOLDOWN_MS`/`DOOR_COLOR` for the front door and what it takes to break one, `World.BUILDING_INDEX_PAD` for the chunk-bucketed building lookup and `Player.MAGNET_RADIUS`/`MAGNET_SPEED_START`/`MAGNET_SPEED_MAX`/`MAGNET_ACCEL`/`MAGNET_CATCH` for the loot magnet; `Entities.LOOT_*` for the ground halo and bob under dropped loot; `MonsterArt`, everything drawn on a monster above its silhouette (the ground shadow, the idle breath, the eye glow and how far it flares once the player is noticed, a wraith's alpha drift, the colour of a monster's rusted steel), with `MonsterKind.shape`/`weapon`/`eye_color` naming which silhouette a kind gets, what it visibly carries and what colour its eyes are (`BossKind` carries the same three); `CritterKind`/`CRITTER_KINDS` one entry per animal (look, hp, `temperament`, `min_distance`, `group`, sprint/stamina, bite damage, drop) with `CRITTER_KINDS_BY_NAME` for the ones placed deliberately (the dog), and `Wildlife` the tuning they share: spawn/despawn distances, wander idle, the flee model (`FLEE_TURN_RATE_DEG`/`TIRED_MULT`/`RECOVER_MS`/`BOLT_DURATION_MS`), `BREAK_OFF_HP_FRAC`, `PACK_AGGRO_RADIUS` and how many dogs a village or camp keeps; `World.FREE_SPOT_MAX_RINGS`, how far `World.free_spot_near` searches for somewhere to stand, and `World.CAMP_SEARCH_RINGS`, how far `World.find_bandit_camp` generates outward looking for a camp to send a quest at; `Player.REGEN_RATE`/`REGEN_DELAY_MS` and `Stats.VITALITY_REGEN_PER_LEVEL`, kept deliberately low so healing up is a between-fights thing; `Decals` splat sizes plus the kill-spray fan tuning
- `src/core/save.py`: `SaveSystem`, atomic thread-safe JSON save system; background generators persist on completion via `save_all` (keys: `context`, `coins`, `weapon_bar` (the ids the number keys reach for, separate from `equipped`, which is what is drawn right now), `potion_bar` (the ids the quick keys drink), `active_melee` (which melee slot is in hand), `name_buffer`, `used_names`, `breakables`, `pois` (a dict of touched-POI state by id, not a list of POIs), `villages` (every settlement generated so far; its buildings and people live in `buildings`/`npcs`), `traps` (which bear traps have shut), `tunnels` (what is left of each well's tunnel) and `underground` (the tunnel the player was standing in when the game was saved, so a game saved down there loads down there), `explored` (the "gx:gy" cells the minimap has lit), `camp_rest` (when each fire or villager's bed will serve the player again), `death_taunts` (death-screen lines written ahead of need), `buffs`, plus player/world state)
- `src/core/camera.py`: `Camera`, world to screen coordinate translation; `ScreenShake`/`get_shake` global camera-shake state applied in the translation
- `src/core/screen_fx.py`: `Hitstop`/`get_hitstop`, a brief freeze-frame (slows gameplay dt, not render dt) triggered on crits/kills/boss deaths; `HurtVignette`/`get_vignette`, a red screen-edge flash triggered when the player takes damage
- `src/core/damage_fx.py`: the half of a breakable's feedback that lives on the prop instead of in the air. `get_damage_fx()` is a session-only registry of recent blows keyed by a *string* (a crate or a window is an index into a building's layout, not an object), read back by the drawing code as a flinch `offset` away from the blow and a `flash`; `draw_cracks` is the wear itself, seeded from world space and growing as the hit-point pool empties, so how battered something looks is how close it is to giving. Called by `WorldCombat._prop_chip` on the way in and by `breakables.py`/`buildings.py`/`poi.py` on the way out
- `src/core/swing_arcs.py`: `SwingArc`/`SwingArcSystem`/`get_swings`, the trail a melee attack leaves, spawned by `WorldCombat.handle_attack` over exactly the wedge (`WeaponArchetype.arc_deg` at the weapon's reach) the hit test accepts, wider and hotter for a cleaving weapon; a `pierce_melee` weapon is tested down a lane rather than across a wedge, so it draws that lane instead (`spawn_thrust`/`ThrustTrail`, from the end of the blind spot out to where the hit test stops). Session-only and updated in `Game.run` like particles and floating text; the picture and the hit test read the same two numbers, so the arc can never promise reach the swing does not have
- `src/core/impact_fx.py`: `ImpactRing`/`ImpactFxSystem`/`get_impacts`, the picture an area effect leaves: a ring expanding to exactly the radius the damage was applied over and a jagged bolt out to everything it caught. Session-only and updated in `Game.run` like the swing arcs, and drawn beside them. Chain Strike used to pop three damage numbers across the screen with nothing connecting them, which reads as a bug; anything new that hurts a crowd at once draws itself through here rather than trusting the numbers to explain it
- `src/core/daynight.py`: `DayNightCycle`, owned by `World` (`world.daynight`), tracks elapsed time within a repeating cycle and exposes `darkness`/`is_night`/`phase` (the "Day"/"Dusk"/"Night"/"Dawn" word the minimap's clock spells out) and `time_until(progress)`, how long until the cycle next reaches a given point, which is what sleeping through to dawn has to skip; `draw` overlays an ambient night tint outdoors (only, drawn from `Game.run()` next to `get_vignette().draw`), blended toward the blood night's red by `EventSystem.blood_intensity` rather than swapped for it, so the event comes on and bleeds out; `is_night` also speeds up monster respawn in `World.update` (weaker than, and not stacked with, the blood night respawn multiplier)
- `src/core/decals.py`: `Decal`, `DecalSystem`/`get_decals`, capped session-only ground blood splats (small on a hit, bigger "pool" on a kill), drawn beneath entities in both outdoor and interior rendering; `spawn_spray` throws a fan of droplets out along the killing blow (thinning out with distance), used by `World._spill_blood` so a death leaves a directional mess instead of one tidy circle
- `src/core/floating_text.py`: `FloatingText`, `FloatingTextSystem`/`get_floating_text`, rising/fading damage numbers popping over a hit (bigger and gold on a crit)
- `src/core/utils.py`: `ConversationHistory`, random color/coordinate helpers, `parse_shop_inventories` / `parse_response_quest_analysis` (LLM response parsing; a response that won't parse ends as "no quest" and is recorded through `llm_log.log_parse_failure` rather than printed and lost). The quest parser repairs the model's near-JSON by quoting every unquoted value, so booleans arrive as text and go through `_as_bool`: plain `bool()` read `"false"` as true and handed over quests the player had refused
- `src/core/dialogue_log.py`: `write_conversation`, persists finished NPC conversations to Markdown files under `logs/dialogues/`
- `src/core/llm_log.py`: `log_call` and `log_parse_failure`, appending every LLM generation (any category, streaming or not) as a JSON line to `logs/llm_calls.jsonl`, with prompts, response, duration, and token counts, for later quality/speed analysis
- `src/core/particles.py`: `Particle`, `ParticleSystem`, world-space particle bursts for combat/pickup feedback; `spawn_burst` (omnidirectional) and `spawn_directional_burst` (cone away from an angle) both take an optional `gravity` (fake-z arc, so a particle launches up and settles back onto the ground plane instead of floating forever) and `shape` ("circle" or a rotating "shard", for wood/glass debris)
- `src/core/audio.py`: `SoundManager`, procedural sound effects synthesised in memory (no audio asset files)

## Architecture notes

- The LLM runs on a background thread via `LLMRequestQueue`. Never call `llama_cpp` directly from the main thread.
- Healing is scarce on purpose, and each source has one job: potions mid-fight, a campfire for a partial patch-up once per fire per cooldown, a bed for a genuine full rest (no bed anywhere is bought: an inn room is taken exactly as a villager's bed is, at the price of being seen and of that bed going cold for `REST_COOLDOWN_S`), and a slow passive trickle that only starts `Player.REGEN_DELAY_MS` after the last hit. Anything that hands back full health for free (the campfire used to) collapses the whole difficulty curve, so new healing goes through one of those four rather than beside them. A bed comes in two forms and they cost different things: the inn's takes coins, a villager's own takes the risk of being seen and puts that household on the same `REST_COOLDOWN_S` a campfire gets, so a street of houses is not a row of free full heals. A bed also costs time, which is the other half of what it is for: sleeping runs the world forward to dawn (`Game._sleep_until_dawn` over `World.pass_time`), so the night is something the player can put behind them at the price of whatever they had drunk, and it is refused outright while anything hostile is close, exactly as a campfire is.
- The spawn point is protected ground, and it is protected in three ways at once because any one of them alone still gets the player killed on arrival. Nothing hostile is *spawned* within `World.SAFE_RADIUS` of the world centre (`_spawn_is_sheltered`, which also covers every settlement plus a margin), so the starting town has no ring of monsters waiting one screen out; the player is *placed* by `World.safe_spot_near`, which looks for hostiles as well as walls, with `clear_hostiles_around` first sending whatever chased them there back out; and they arrive with `Player.invuln_until` open for `Death.SPAWN_GRACE_S`, spent early the moment they attack. How crowded the world is at all follows from where the player is standing (`World.roaming_cap`), not from a world-wide constant: the ground around the starting town is deliberately thin and the deep wilds are full. Monsters may still *wander* into any of this; what is forbidden is being stood up there.
- Difficulty is distance from the world centre, and it is three levers pulled together rather than one. *Which* kinds spawn: `MonsterKind.min_distance` unlocks a kind and `Entities.DEPTH_HALF_LIFE` fades it out again, so the mix gets harder rather than merely wider as the player walks out. *How many*: `World.roaming_cap`, eased over a long ramp out to `ROAMING_CAP_FAR_DISTANCE`. *What has a name*: `Boss.LANDMARK_MIN_DISTANCE` puts the first world's guardian out on the far side of the settled ring and `Boss.ROAM_MIN_DISTANCE` (which quest bosses are also placed from, in a band outward) keeps every other boss well past it. Night pulls the first lever and only the first: `DayNight.NIGHT_DANGER_BONUS` rolls a spawn as if the ground were deeper, capped at `NIGHT_DANGER_DISTANCE_FRAC` of how far out it already is, so the dark deepens the wilds instead of importing them onto the starting town's doorstep. A camp leader (`CAMP_LEADER_DANGER_BONUS`) and a tunnel garrison (`Tunnels.GUARD_DANGER_BONUS`) use that same bonus, so anything that stretches the `min_distance` ladder has to stretch those with it. Nothing scales a monster's own stat block: a Troll is the same Troll wherever it is met, which is what keeps `MONSTER_KINDS` the one source of what a creature is worth.
- Night is a state of the world, not a property of what spawned in it. `World.night_damage_mult` and the detection multiplier are read per frame and passed into `Monster.move`, so a monster that spawned at noon still hits like a night monster after dark; only the danger bonus a new spawn rolls with is baked in at spawn time.
- An animal's behaviour is its species' `temperament`, nothing else. `CritterKind.temperament` ("passive"/"retaliate"/"predator"/"guard") is the single switch behind whether a `Critter` runs, fights back, hunts on sight or takes its village's side, and `Critter._should_hunt` is the one place that reads it. Adding an animal means adding a row to `CRITTER_KINDS`, not a subclass and not a new branch in `World.update`. Dogs are that table's one deliberately placed kind (weight 0): a village or camp stands them up and owns them, which is why they carry `village_key`/`camp_id` while wildlife carries neither.
- A monster's look is its kind's `shape`, not its colour. Every hostile thing used to be the same circle with two smaller circles for arms, which meant a slime, a skeleton and an ogre differed only in radius and hue; now `MonsterKind.shape` picks a silhouette out of `monster_art._SHAPES` and `weapon` puts something recognisable in its hand, drawn by the same `gear.draw_weapon` the player's gear goes through. Adding a monster means adding a row to `MONSTER_KINDS` naming an existing shape, or one function to `monster_art.py` and one entry in `_SHAPES` when it needs a new one: nothing about a creature's look belongs in `monsters.py`, and no kind may be told apart by colour alone. What sits above the silhouette is deliberately shared by all of them (shadow, breath, eyes), because those three are what get read first and they must not vary per kind.
- Violence against a villager is a whole-settlement event, and it is now a timed one. Any blow landing on an NPC goes through `WorldCombat._resolve_npc_hit`, which calls `World.provoke_village`: every NPC inside that village's radius turns hostile at once, their affinity floors and their quests are dropped. That anger runs on a clock (`Villages.ANGER_S`, extended by each new offence up to `ANGER_CAP_S`, counted down on the minimap's village strip) and expires back to `Affinity.FORGIVEN`, so a brawl is something a village lives down. A death is not: killing a villager calls `World.hold_grudge` instead, which sets `NPC.grudge` on the whole settlement and no clock ever clears it. Quests dropped by a provocation stay dropped, and `EventSystem._generate_crisis` refuses to hand a new one out in an angry village, which was the last path that still did. Theft is the one exception to the all-or-nothing rule and it has exactly one entry point of its own: `Game._check_witness` asks `World.theft_witness` who could see the player empty a chest or climb into a bed, and `World.catch_thief` turns that one villager, alone, while the rest of the settlement goes on with its day. Being seen is a field of view with walls in it, not a radius: `NPC.sees` tests `Crime.VIEW_CONE_DEG` off the villager's own facing and `World.can_see` then walks the line for anything solid (`sight_reach`), with exactly one thing see-through, the building the player is standing in, since the villager being robbed is on the other side of a doorway rather than behind a wall and a strict test would make every theft free; `GameRenderer._draw_witness_cones` draws exactly that wedge on the ground while a chest or a bed is in reach, and the prompt names whoever is currently watching, so waiting for a back to be turned is the mechanic. Its price is that a villager stops turning to greet the player while the player is inside a building (`face_player`), since a cone that always points at you is not a cone. Escalation is the player's own doing: swinging back at whoever caught them lands in `_resolve_npc_hit` like any other blow and turns the whole village. No other path may turn a single NPC hostile. The second exception is not the player's doing at all: a monster's stray arrow can kill a villager, and `_resolve_npc_hit(by_player=False)` skips the provocation, because the village has nothing to blame the player for.
- A crowd surrounds, it does not queue. Anything chasing the same target goes through `World.assign_surround_slots`, which deals the chasers evenly spaced bearings round it and hands out only `Entities.MAX_ACTIVE_ATTACKERS` permissions to swing, nearest first; the rest hold their place on the ring and walk it slowly round (`Monster.circle_side`). That is the one lever for how overwhelming a pack feels, and it is deliberately two numbers rather than a per-kind behaviour: raising the token count makes every fight in the game bloodier, and nothing else has to know. The same call serves an angry village, which is why `NPC` carries a `slot_angle` and an `attack_token` at all: a dozen villagers each landing a blow on the same frame was a woodchipper rather than a fight.
- A town is walled and the wall is one thing, not a new system. `Village.defences()` is a ring set outside the last house, with a gate in the middle of each side, a gatehouse either side of every gateway and a tower at each corner, solid through the same `Village.blocks` the well already went through and bucketed by chunk with it (`World._village_solids_by_chunk`), and its stretches are handed to `World._detour_corner` (`walls_near`) so a chaser routes round one to a gate instead of grinding on it: every stretch runs from a corner tower to a gatepost, so rounding its end *is* walking to the nearest way in. A gate on every side is deliberate, so walling a town in never turns an approach into a dead end. Who stands on it is not a new kind of person either: `World._post_guards` puts an ordinary villager at each gate, tower and wall stretch with `is_guard` set, which only means they always take up arms (`NPC.is_militia`), carry a real weapon and hold their post instead of strolling. Only `Villages.WALLED_SIZES` (and the starting town) get any of it: a hamlet has nothing to defend with.

- How well defended a settlement is is one number, and it is decided by where it stands. `Village.tier` is rolled once from the distance to the world centre and the size, then persisted with the village like its wall, and *everything* that makes a deep wilds town different from a border hamlet reads it: the wall's material and thickness, its towers' size, how many guards stand on it, whether any of them carry a bow, whether there are stakes and a ditch outside it, which weapon ladder its militia and guards draw from, and how much health its people have. Adding a new difference between settlements means adding a row indexed by tier, not a new flag. It is the same idea as `MonsterKind.min_distance`, pointed at a place rather than at a creature: walking further out should be visible before anything is fought.

- A village is made strong in numbers, in health and behind its wall, never in what its people are carrying. A farmer owns no weapon: `Entities.VILLAGER_WEAPONS` is a shelf of tools, all of it resolving to the `tool` archetype (short, slow, feeble), and the fallback for an unrecognised name is deliberately still the sword, which is why a tool needs a keyword rather than just a name. What buys the strength back is `World._set_toughness` (health per tier and per role, the one place a villager's health is set), `Villages.VILLAGERS_PER_HOME_BY_SIZE` and the wall itself. A villager who hits harder is the one change that is not allowed: it turns every street into a fight the player cannot walk away from.

- A gate is the only part of a wall that gives, for the same reason a door is the only part of a house that does. The stretches, the towers and the gatehouses are never breakable: there is a gate on every side and `_detour_corner` routes round the wall already, so nothing is ever unable to reach anything. What a gate answers is a question the wall created: `World._bar_gates` shuts a settlement's gates while it is angry at the player, which is when the player is inside a town that wants them dead, and `WorldCombat._hit_gate`/`bash_gates` let them hack their way out (or a pack beat its way in) on the same hit-point pool a front door uses. `gate_broken` is persisted, `gate_hp` is not, exactly as a door does it.

- A building is one rect or two, and the second one is an L's wing. `Building.footprint()` is what it is built of, `bounds` is the box that fits it (the chunk index, chase detours and the tree and bear-trap clearances all ask for that), and `rect` stays the main block the facade hangs on, so the door, the windows, the awning and the doorstep never learn about wings. The two halves are joined by exactly one rect, `_wing_opening`: subtracted from the wall shell it is the way through, taken as a floor it is what makes `interior_rects` one connected space, which is the same trick a tunnel's corridors use. Furniture then has to respect it: `_RoomSpace.keep_clear` holds every way through a room (the corridor in from the door, the neck of an L), `add` nudges a fixed piece out of one and `try_place` rolls over every floor, because a table dropped in the neck walls half a building off and a wing nobody can walk into is worse than no wing at all.
- A villager fights with whatever they own, and what they own is a name. `NPC.weapon_name` is rolled off the same home seed the militia flag is, out of three pools (a tool for a farmer, a weapon for the militia, a soldier's weapon for a guard), and `weapon` resolves it through the ordinary `weapon_archetype`: reach, damage and cadence all come off the table the player's own weapons use, and the thing is drawn in their hand through the same `gear.draw_weapon`. Adding a villager weapon is a name in a tuple, not a branch in `NPC._hunt`.
- A village defends itself, and who does the defending is decided per person. A monster that walks onto a settlement's grounds is retargeted by `World._monster_target` onto the nearest villager instead of filing past them toward the player (a camp guard is exempt: it holds ground rather than raids). `World.militia_orders` then works out once a frame what everyone does about it: whoever `NPC.is_militia` says takes up arms goes to meet it, everyone else runs for the nearest door and shuts it behind them. Villagers killed in that fight are dead for good, resolved as `by_player=False` so the village blames nobody and the player is paid nothing. `Monster.move` and `NPC.update` both hand their swing's damage back rather than applying it, because only the world knows whether a blow goes through the player's shield or off a villager's health, and that is what let one chase routine serve both.
- A weapon family answers a question; it is not a rung on a ladder. Rarity and affixes already carry the numbers, so `WEAPON_ARCHETYPES` progresses sideways: the spear pays a blind spot and buys a lane through a line of chasers plus a shove that puts whatever closed back out at the end of its shaft, the pole barely hurts and moves people (into a river, onto a bear trap, off a ring that has closed round the player), the boomerang costs no ammo and only one is ever in the air, and a staff's element (fire/frost/storm, read out of the weapon's own name by `weapon_archetype`) points an existing mechanic at whatever the bolt hits rather than adding one. A new family belongs in that table naming what it is for; a weapon that is only a bigger number is a rarity roll, not a family.
- A cleaving weapon is worth aiming. `WorldCombat._cleave_falloff` scales what each target takes by how far it is off the facing and off the reach, down to `Combat.CLEAVE_MIN` at the edges, and the swing then carries on into the hostile groups beneath the one it engaged (a monster and the angry villager beside it both take it) while never touching the peaceful ones, so hunting an animal in a crowded street still cannot start a brawl. The drawn arc is unchanged: it says what the swing covers, and the damage numbers now say what each part of that cover was worth.
- Nothing the player did not do pays the player. Friendly fire (a monster's arrow catching another monster, an animal or a villager) resolves through the same `_resolve_*_hit` methods with `by_player=False`, and that flag is the single switch for every reward and consequence attached to a kill: xp, hitstop, lootbox, quest counters, pack aggro, the village turning. The kill itself always happens; only the credit is withheld. Three things deliberately survive an uncredited kill, because they are world state rather than reward: a bandit camp's garrison count (`on_guard_killed`), the body's dropped item, and (since coins became an object) the purse beside it, both of which are lying on the ground for anyone to take.
- Nothing in the world breaks in a single hit. Furniture, crates, barrels, pots, bushes, windows, doors and wilderness caches all take the swing's damage off a hit-point pool and only give on the blow that empties it. Where that pool lives follows what the thing already saves: a `Breakable` persists its own `hp`, a building's furniture/windows keep theirs session-only (the save records what broke, not how far along the rest are). Every stick of furniture in a room comes apart, not only the crates (`Buildings.FURNITURE_HP` per kind), because a room where one box out of six reacts to a sword reads as scenery with a bug in it; the bed and the chest are the exceptions, both being mechanics the player would only be removing from themselves. Wrecking somebody's room is a crime like emptying their chest (`World.report_crime`), which is why the witness cones are on the ground the whole time the player is standing indoors rather than only over a chest, and a POI's `cache_hp` is session-only because a POI is rebuilt from its chunk seed anyway. The pool is never invisible: every one of them draws its own wear through `core/damage_fx.py`, so a prop that is nearly gone looks nearly gone, and a blow that did not finish something still visibly landed on it.
- A door is the one obstacle a monster may break, because it is the one that cannot be walked round. Every door starts shut and is part of the wall shell while it is (`Building._wall_segments`), the player opens and shuts it with E, and a chaser that ends up on the wrong side of one either lets itself in (a villager, `World.open_door_for`: it is their street) or beats it down (`WorldCombat.bash_doors`, several swings, audible, permanent once through). Nothing else about the world may become breakable to let a monster take a shortcut: if a monster cannot reach the player, the answer is navigation, not demolition.
- The creeper is the same blast with nobody holding it: it walks up, plants itself and burns a visible fuse, so the answer is to kill it, shove it or leave the ring it has drawn. Nothing it kills pays the player, the bear trap's rule, because the reward for a creeper is killing it before the fuse runs out. The one exception to "everything breakable is loot or scenery" is the powder keg, and it is there to answer a different question: how do you kill a crowd without a better weapon? `WorldCombat.explode` hurts everything near it, the player included, chains into other kegs, and pays nothing at all, so a keg is a piece of ground worth fighting over rather than another barrel. It is also the only prop a projectile interacts with: shooting one from across a clearing is the plan the mechanic exists for.
- `World` is one class split across four files by mixin: `world.py` owns the state, `combat.py` (`WorldCombat`) resolves blows, `streaming.py` (`WorldStreaming`) builds the map, `places.py` (`WorldPlaces`) is what happens at a place the player has walked to (camps, fires, shrines, directions, theft, the explored-cell memory). They all work on the same `self.monsters`/`self.items`/`self.pois`, which is the point: the entity lists stay a single source of truth, and the file you open is decided by what you are changing, not by which object holds the data. Adding a method means picking the file by that same rule, not defaulting to `world.py`.
- `src/` is the package root; all imports are relative to it (e.g. `import core.constants as c`).
- No tests exist; skip the pre-push hook accordingly.
- Don't launch the game (`uv run game`, or any script that opens a pygame window) to verify a change, and don't ask Valentin to launch it and report back. It pops a real window on his live desktop. State what changed and stop; he'll test it himself if he wants to.
- To self-check a rendering/UI change, a throwaway script that imports just the drawing code, stubs out heavy objects, and renders to an offscreen `Surface`/PNG is fine, but set `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy` before `pygame.init()` in it. Without that, `pygame.display.set_mode(...)` pops a real window on Valentin's desktop too, same problem as launching the game.
- A building's interior is just its own footprint in world space, not a separate coordinate space or screen: `Building.interior_rect()` is the footprint inset by `Buildings.WALL_THICKNESS`, and `Building.blocks()` collides against a thin wall shell (with a permanent door-sized gap) plus furniture, instead of treating the whole footprint as one solid block. `Game.interior` is recomputed every frame from the player's position (`world.building_at`) rather than tracked as an enter/exit mode switch; there is no teleport, no separate indoor monster/projectile lists, and `World.update`/`World.handle_attack` always run against the one `world.monsters`/`world.items`/`world.projectiles`. `GameRenderer.draw_world` draws every building normally except the one the player is standing in, which `Building.draw(..., player_inside=True)` renders as a roofless cutaway (floor, walls, furniture) so the rest of the map keeps drawing around it in the same pass.
- Item persistence works by id: `SaveSystem` stores `inventory`/`equipped` as item id lists and `items` as the one master list of full item dicts (see the key list in `src/core/save.py`). Anything handed to the player has to end up in `world.items` or its id won't resolve on reload and the item silently vanishes. For items that are granted instantly (chest, lootbox) rather than picked up off the ground, they're appended already flagged `picked_up = True`, purely so the id resolves later; they never render or get picked up again.
- `Building.dropped_items` (loot popped from a smashed crate, waiting to be picked up) is session-only and not written to the save file. A `Building` object itself lives for the whole process, so e.g. dropped crate loot survives leaving and re-entering the room, but not a save/reload.
- The world has no edge. `World.WORLD_SIZE` is only the settled area: it fixes the starting village, the spawn point, boss roaming ranges and the distance-from-center that scales monster difficulty. Past it the map keeps going, and points of interest, villages and floor details all stream in per chunk, so walking out never runs into a wall of nothing. Because a POI is regenerated from its chunk seed on every visit, it must stay deterministic from `(cx, cy)` alone: anything the player changes goes in `World.poi_state`, keyed by the POI id, and nothing else about a POI is saved.
- Villages are the exception to that rule, deliberately. `village_site(cx, cy)` (where a settlement goes) is a pure function of the coordinates like everything else streamed, but the settlement itself is generated **once** and then persisted with the world, buildings in `buildings` and people in `npcs`, exactly like the starting town. Regenerating it per visit would mean rebuilding NPC names, affinity, quests and LLM shop stock from a seed, which is impossible. So: the map is endless and deterministic, what stands on it is generated on demand and kept. New villages must go through `World._ensure_village` so their buildings reach the chunk index (`_index_buildings`) and `set_active_buildings`.
- The wilderness is terrain, not entities. Everything in `scenery.py` (trees, boulders, grass, ponds, roads, ground patches) is generated from its chunk's coordinates, held only while that chunk is loaded and never saved, exactly like the floor details: nothing the player does can change a single piece of it, so there is no state to persist and no id to key. A chunk rolls **one biome** and lays out that biome's clumps, which is what makes a wood a wood; scattering every kind evenly over every chunk gives texture, not places. The only part that touches gameplay is collision: trunks and boulders answer `World.blocked` through their own fine grid (`Scenery.INDEX_CELL`, finer than the chunk grid the buildings use, because a wood holds far more trunks than a village holds houses), which is enough for the player, monsters, critters, arrows and spawning all at once, since they already go through `blocked`. Monsters get round a trunk by the lookahead in `Monster._steer`; `World._detour_corner` stays about buildings only, a tree being too small to be worth a detour.
- Loot is collected by walking, not by a key. `Game._sweep_loot` runs once a frame: anything on the ground within `Player.MAGNET_RADIUS` accelerates at the player and is taken on contact, which is why `Interaction` has no "item" kind any more and E belongs to doors, beds, chests and people alone. Two things it refuses to do: pull an item that is still hopping out of whatever dropped it (`start_pop_anim`), so a purse is seen to fall off the body before it flies at you, and pull anything standing on another building's floor, the same rule `GameRenderer._hidden_indoors` draws by, because loot must not come out through a wall.
- Coins on the ground are an object, not a number. A killed villager's purse is an `Item` of type `"coins"` holding its amount in `quantity`, dropped where the body fell and credited (through `gain_coins`, so the coin-find accessory still applies) only when somebody walks into it; it never enters the inventory and leaves the master item list the moment it is taken. That is what lets an uncredited kill still leave money lying there, the way the possession already did, and it is the shape any new coin drop should take rather than another instant credit.
- Quests are the best coins in the game, and everything else was trimmed around that. The band a quest pays is the game's decision, not the model's: `c.QUEST_COIN_BANDS` per quest type clamps whatever figure the NPC named in their parting line, the prompt is told that band so the sentence and the payout agree, and the hardest types always hand over gear as well (`Quests.ALWAYS_ITEM_TYPES`). Against that, salvage is pocket money: shops pay `Stats.SELL_BASE` of a low `items.base_value` with a ceiling under 1.0, and lootbox/crate/cache coins are all small. New income belongs on the quest and cache side of that line, not on the "sell everything you find" side.
- A bear trap is the only thing in the world that stops something moving without a wall in the way. `Entity.root(ms)` (and `Critter`'s own copy of it) is a timestamp every mover checks in its own step routine, so a rooted thing still turns, still swings, still shoots and still bleeds; it simply does not get anywhere. Nobody owns a trap and nobody aimed it: `WorldCombat.snap_traps` tests the player, monsters, wildlife and villagers against the same `catches`, the first one to stand on it springs it, and it resolves `by_player=False` because the player did not set it. That is also the whole of what makes it a tactic rather than a hazard: the seconds it buys are usable by whoever is not in it.
- The underground is not a second world, it is a far corner of this one. A tunnel's rooms are ordinary world-space rectangles at `Tunnels.ORIGIN` plus its village chunk's slot, so the player, the monsters, the projectiles, the loot and the save all work down there unchanged, exactly as a building's interior is just its own footprint. What makes it read as somewhere else is subtraction, and it hangs off the single `World.underground`: `blocked` answers with the tunnel's rock instead of the map, and `update` stops syncing chunks, stops revealing map cells, stops running events, stops despawning, and stops standing anything up around the player. So nothing streams into a tunnel, nothing wanders in after you, and what is down there was put there when you climbed down. Its garrison is a count like a bandit camp's (tagged `camp_id`, never saved, never counted toward the roaming cap, stood back up on the next descent), its hoard is placed once ever, and dying down there surfaces you like any other death while the tunnel keeps whatever is left of its garrison.
- The first thing seen is the world, and the lore is read before it. The title screen is a live settlement (`MenuScene`) built by the same generator the game uses, with real villagers walking it under a moving sky; opening a session then holds the screen black, writes the world's context onto it with nothing else drawn anywhere, and fades the world up once the player dismisses it (`ContextMenu.intro`/`draw_fade`). That is deliberate: the lore used to open as a panel over a street already full of moving people, where it read as one more widget and went unread. The spawn grace is opened on the first frame the player can actually see the world for the same reason it is granted after the death screen, since the opening holds for as long as the model takes and as long as the player reads.
- Water is the one piece of terrain that is neither a wall nor scenery: it is a speed penalty. Nothing is stopped by a river, a pond or a lake (`blocked` never sees them), everything is slowed in one through `World.terrain_speed`, and a bridge over it takes the point back to ordinary ground (`World.water_at`, reading the same fine grid the trunks use). Only the player ever gets better at it: `Stats.swim_multiplier` climbs from `Scenery.SWIM_SPEED` toward `SWIM_SPEED_MAX` as the swimming stat trains, and never reaches walking pace, so a river stays a real answer to being chased and a bridge stays the fast way over for the whole game. That is why navigation needed no changes: nothing has to route around water, it crosses slowly. Rivers are generated like roads (a pure function of the lane index) and bend around settlements rather than being cut off at them; landmarks and cover keep out of the water instead.
- An angry village fights the way it defends itself. The split `NPC.is_militia` makes about a monster in the street decides what the mob does about the player too (`World._mob_orders`): the militia close and swing, everybody else keeps `Villages.MOB_STANDOFF` back and throws stones, which is a real threat in numbers and cannot be answered with a sword. A mob also breaks: anyone cut to `Villages.ROUT_HP_FRAC` (`NPC.routed`) leaves the fight for the nearest door, so a village thins out as it loses instead of feeding farmers into a blade one at a time. Nothing here is a new kind of villager, only a new thing to point the existing split at.
- Nothing on legs slides across the ground. `Gait` (game/entities/entities.py) is one walk cycle advanced by the distance actually covered rather than by the clock, held by every `Entity` and by `Critter`, and read once per frame by whatever draws the thing. So a rooted monster, a villager pinned against a wall, an animal wading a river and a corpse all stop animating for free, nothing has to be told how fast it is going, and the arms, the legs, the body bob and a monster's rocking all come off the same number. New artwork that moves reads that number; it does not keep an animation clock of its own.
- Nothing walks the whole building list per frame. Buildings and village wells are bucketed by chunk (`World._index_buildings`), and collision (`blocked`, `building_at`), swing/window tests, chase detours and the renderer all go through `buildings_near`/`buildings_in_range`. The list only grows as the player finds villages, so a full scan would make the game slower the more of the world had been seen.
- Exactly one interaction prompt is on screen at a time. `Game.current_interaction` picks the nearest interactable in reach (NPC, campfire, door, interior chest/bed, well, ladder), `Game._interact` runs that same one, and `GameRenderer._draw_interaction_prompt` draws it. Never draw a prompt from the entity/building that owns the thing: that is what used to stack a label over every bed in the tavern, and what let a prompt point at something other than what the key did.
- The weapon bar sits on top of the equip slots, it does not replace them. `Player.weapon_bar` is a short list of weapon ids the number keys reach for; pressing one calls `select_weapon`, which either makes an already-equipped weapon the one in hand or equips it into the slot its archetype belongs to. So switching a bow leaves the swords equipped, and nothing in `combat.py` knows the bar exists.
- Two melee weapons are worn and one of them swings. `Player.active_melee` indexes `MELEE_SLOTS`, X swaps it, and *nothing* reads a melee slot by name: combat, the affix helpers, `weapon_bonus` and the drawn `gear()` all go through `active_melee_weapon()`, so carrying a spear beside a sword is a stance chosen before the fight rather than a trip through the inventory during it. Equipping a melee weapon fills the free slot before displacing the one in hand, and emptying the slot in hand falls back to the other one, because a player standing next to their own spare should never be barehanded.
- What a key reaches for is a choice the player made, and it is saved. The weapon bar (`weapon_bar`, number keys) and the potion quickbar (`potion_bar`, `Potions.QUICK_KEYS`) are both lists of item ids, both filled automatically only into a *free* slot (on pickup, through `Player._auto_slot`) and both reassigned by hand by right-clicking the item in the inventory. Picking up a nicer-sounding elixir can therefore never push the healing potion off the bar. Arrows are the one thing equipped outright on pickup, and only when nothing is loaded: firing is the point of carrying them, and `ready_ammo` falls back to the cheapest stack anyway.
- The game saves itself, and the player can see it happen. `Game.save_data` is the one path to disk: a 5 minute timer (`World.AUTOSAVE_INTERVAL_S`) plus the moments worth not replaying (a tunnel entered or left, a night slept through, a quest handed in, a death, quitting), each of them pushing the timer back. Every one of them stamps `Game.last_save_ms`, which `GameRenderer._draw_save_marker` shows as a brief disc in the corner. A new save trigger belongs on that list rather than in a new write path.
- An item's icon is derived, never stored as a decision. `items.icon_shape(item_type, name)` is the single source of the shape, `Item.__init__` sets it from there and `Item.from_dict` recomputes it rather than restoring the saved value, so an old save picks up new artwork instead of keeping a stale silhouette. Nothing about an item's identity or behaviour lives in `shape`.
- Particles, floating damage numbers and blood decals are each one global session-only system (`get_particles`/`get_floating_text`/`get_decals`), updated once per frame in `Game.run()` rather than inside `World.update`, so they keep animating even while a menu pauses the rest of the update. They're drawn from `GameRenderer.draw_world`, same call whether the player is indoors or out.
- A bandit camp's garrison is a number, not a set of monsters. `PointOfInterest.guards_alive`/`leader_alive` are persisted with the camp; `_populate_camp` stands that many bandits up when the chunk loads, `_unload_chunk` takes them away with it, `World.serialize` never saves them, and `on_guard_killed` is the only thing that lowers the count. So a camp costs the same whether the player has found one or a hundred, killing four of five guards survives walking away and reloading, and clearing a camp is recorded rather than inferred from who happens to be loaded. Guards are tagged `Monster.camp_id` (with `camp_leader` for the one worth remembering), which is what keeps them out of the distance despawn, out of the roaming population cap and out of the save. They come back at full health, which is the one thing the count deliberately forgets.
- A merchant's shelf is a clock, not a one-off. `NPC.restock_at` is persisted wall time, `World._restock_merchants` tops the stock back up to `Villages.SHOP_STOCK_TARGET` when it runs out, and the shop menu counts it down so buying a merchant out is a decision with a known price in time. The delivery is rolled locally through `loot.roll_shop_stock`: one LLM call per shop per restock is exactly the cost the batched generation in `merchant_system.py` exists to avoid, and what is already on the shelf is never replaced, only added to.
- The map is memory, not radar. `World.explored` is the only thing that lights the minimap up, it is filled in by walking (`_reveal_around`) and persisted, and the minimap draws no live entity of any kind at a fixed small zoom. Anything that would let the player scout from the HUD instead of walking (monster dots, a full-screen map, zoom) is a deliberate omission, not a missing feature. The single exception is a rumour's mark (`World.mark_rumor`/`rumor_marks`): somewhere the player was *told* about, held session-only and rubbed out on arrival, which is what turns a rumour into somewhere to go. It marks a place, never a creature, and nothing else may put a pin on the map.
- `Hitstop` (`core/screen_fx.py`) only slows the `dt` fed to gameplay updates (player/monster movement, `World.update`, projectiles); camera shake, particles, floating text and decals keep advancing on the real `dt` so the freeze reads as a snap rather than the whole frame stalling.
