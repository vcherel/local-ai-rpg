# rpg-ai

A 2D open-world RPG where all AI runs locally. NPCs generate dialogue via an LLM, quests are created dynamically from conversations, and the world context is AI-generated at startup.

## How to run

```bash
uv run game
```

Requires CUDA drivers and the model at `models/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Q2_K.gguf`. See README for setup.

## File map

One line per file. Update this when adding, removing, or substantially repurposing a file, this list is what keeps lookups fast instead of requiring a codebase search.

`saves/save.json`: persisted game state (gitignored). `models/`: GGUF model files (gitignored).

### rpg_ai
- `src/rpg_ai/__main__.py`: entry point. Initialises Pygame and the LLM queue, then loops from main menu to game, building a fresh `SaveSystem` per session (never shared with the previous game, whose generation threads may still be finishing) and clearing it on "New Game"; exits when `Game.quit_app` says the window was closed

### game
- `src/game/game.py`: `Game` class, main loop, input handling, state orchestration; `_handle_key` is the whole in-world key map as one table (after the two ranges that read a key rather than name it: the weapon bar's number keys and the potion quickbar's letters), so a rebinding is one line there and one in `HelpMenu.CONTROLS`; `_pop_levelups` drains `player.stats.pending_levelups` each frame and pops the gold floating text/particle burst/chime over the player; `_respawn` handles death inside the run (penalty, heal, back to world spawn, brief death screen) so dying never bounces the player to the main menu, and `run()` closes the world and the name generator on the way out so their background threads stop writing to the save; `current_interaction` returns the single `Interaction` E acts on right now (nearest of: loot on the ground, a merchant/villager to talk to, a campfire to rest at, a shrine to pray at, a door to open or shut, an interior dropped item/chest/bed), which `_interact` dispatches and the renderer draws as the one on-screen prompt, so the prompt and the key can never disagree and a room full of beds can't stack labels; `_talk_to` refuses (and the prompt reads "<name> is busy...") while `llm_busy()` reports work in flight, so a conversation is never opened behind a call that has to finish first; `_use_door` opens or shuts a building's front door (and steps the player clear so shutting one can never wall them into the doorframe), the only way to put something between them and whatever is chasing them; B (`_trade_nearby`) opens a nearby merchant's shop without going through the conversation (both it and `_talk_to` refuse anyone who has turned hostile); `_sleep_in_bed` is the one full rest in the game, healing everything and clearing the death weakness, unlike a campfire: the inn charges coins for it, a villager's own bed asks nothing but goes on `REST_COOLDOWN_S` afterwards and runs `_check_witness` on the way out, the same check `_open_interior_chest` runs after emptying somebody's chest (whoever saw it turns on the player alone, through `World.catch_thief`); `_respawn` also pops a taunt off `DeathTauntGenerator` and reads `Player.last_hit_by`, which is what the death screen mocks the player with; both the initial spawn and `_respawn` place the player through `World.safe_spot_near` so neither starts inside a wall or under whatever killed them (`_respawn` first calls `World.clear_hostiles_around` on the spawn point), and both open the untouchable window with `Player.grant_spawn_grace` (`run()` rather than `__init__` on the way in, so it covers the first seconds of play rather than the model loading; after `run_game_over` rather than before, since the screen holds for seconds of wall-clock time), and `_announce_pickup` toasts every item that reaches the inventory (the F equip prompt when it beats its slot, its name otherwise, since a bow picked up under a stronger staff used to arrive silently)
- `src/game/world.py`: `World` class, the world state everything else reads: the entity lists (NPCs, monsters, bosses, items, POIs, critters), the per-frame `update`, building lookups, bosses, shops and saving. Three jobs live in their own modules and are mixed in (`class World(WorldCombat, WorldStreaming, WorldPlaces)`), so look in `combat.py` for anything about a blow landing, in `streaming.py` for anything about the map coming into being, and in `places.py` for anything the player does at a place once they have walked to it. Here: `persist_world` flushes generated state (context, shops, boss/landmark/village names) to disk when a background thread finishes it, throttled to `World.PERSIST_MIN_INTERVAL_S` (each call serialises the whole world) and skipped once `close()` has ended the session (a thread outliving its game must not write over the next one's save, and `close()` is also what silences `EventSystem`); `start_shop_generation` stocks every merchant still waiting in one batched call and `_shops_generating`/`_landmark_naming`/`_naming_villages` stop a second identical request being queued while the first is in flight, `sync_quest_boss_names` copies a boss's display name onto the slay_boss quest hunting it (the quest links by internal tag, which is never shown); `_index_buildings`/`buildings_near`/`buildings_in_range` bucket buildings (and village wells) by chunk so collision, drawing and swing tests stay flat as the world keeps gaining villages; `chase_waypoint(chaser, player, radius)` is the navigation shared by monsters, bosses and angry villagers: buildings are the only obstacles and each has one door, so a chase across a wall aims for `_door_goal` (line up with the doorway, then step across the threshold, in or out, or come right up against it when the door is shut and there is nothing to do but beat on it) and `_detour_corner` walks round anything in the way by costing both ways round the rect and heading for the first corner of the shorter one, over the buildings around the chaser by default and over the room's own furniture when both are standing in the same one (a corner it cannot stand in is dropped rather than walked at); `open_door_for` is a chasing villager letting themselves into their own street's house, the courtesy a monster is not given; `line_of_sight(x0, y0, x1, y1)` walks a segment through the same `blocked` everything else uses, which is what stops a ranged monster firing at a player it cannot see, `night_damage_mult` is how much harder everything hits after dark, `quest_target` resolves where the tracked quest points (the only thing left with an offscreen arrow), `item_in_reach`/`npc_in_reach` are the shared reach tests behind the interaction prompt and the E/B keys, `_spawn_critter_away_from`/`critter_respawn_timer` keep wildlife populated around the player like monsters but with no combat cap (the species is rolled from the distance to the world center and pack/herd kinds are stood up as a group), `_ensure_village_dogs` tops each nearby settlement back up to its own fixed number of dogs and `aggro_pack` is what turns an animal's kind (or a village's dogs) on the player together when one of them is struck, `blocked` also answers for the wilderness through `scenery_near` (the trunks and boulders bucketed on `Scenery.INDEX_CELL`) and `scenery_ground_in_range`/`scenery_props_in_range` are what the renderer walks (bucketed by chunk, ground kind by kind in draw order), `_signpost_directions` is a signpost reading out the nearest unexplored lead and marking it like a rumour, `free_spot_near` returns the nearest standable point to a coordinate (the fixed spawn point sits inside the starting town's grid about four times in ten, so the player, not the settlement, is what moves) while `safe_spot_near` is the version used to place the *player*, rejecting any point with something hostile within `World.SAFE_SPOT_CLEARANCE` (`hostiles_near`) as well as any point in a wall, since a spot with no wall in it is not safe if what killed the player is standing on it, and `clear_hostiles_around` sends the roaming pack that followed them there back out into the wilds on a respawn (bosses and camp garrisons stay: dying is not a way to be rid of them); `roaming_cap(player)` is how many roaming monsters the world holds right now, ramped by the player's distance from the world centre (`World.ROAMING_CAP_NEAR` on the starting town's doorstep to `ROAMING_CAP_FAR` past `ROAMING_CAP_FAR_DISTANCE`) rather than one world-wide number, and `_spawn_is_sheltered` is the ground nothing hostile is ever spawned on: inside `World.SAFE_RADIUS` of the world centre, or on any settlement's grounds plus `VILLAGE_SPAWN_MARGIN` (`village_at` takes that margin), so standing in town produces no respawns at all and the player has to walk out to find the fight
- `src/game/combat.py`: `WorldCombat`, mixed into `World`: every blow and its aftermath. `handle_attack` is the one entry point for a swing (left click) or a shot (right click), resolving the weapon's archetype into reach/damage/cadence/cleave and then walking one table of target groups in priority order (bosses, monsters, whatever is already fighting back, peaceful wildlife, peaceful villagers, then crates/caches/props/windows), stopping at the first group with anything in reach; `_targets_in_reach` takes the attacker's own position, the weapon's `min_hit_distance` so a spear can't hit what is pressed against the player, and the wedge (`WeaponArchetype.arc_deg`, tested by `_within_arc`) the swing covers, which is the same wedge `core.swing_arcs` draws on screen, so a cleaving weapon visibly sweeps what it is about to hit and never catches anything behind the player; `_strike_*`/`_resolve_*_hit` apply damage, knockback (skipped for `knockback_immune` bosses), crit and death; `_strike_critter`/`_kill_critter`/`_projectile_hits_critter` make wildlife huntable (an animal already biting is struck right after monsters, the peaceful ones after that and before villagers, so a swing meant for a wolf never lands on a fox and hunting in town starts no brawl) and every hit on one goes through `World.aggro_pack`, so wounding one pack animal answers with the pack; `_spill_blood` is the shared gore of any death (pool, directional droplet fan, blood and body-coloured particles), taking the killing blow's direction so the mess points away from the player; `_apply_on_hit_effects`/`_apply_chainstrike` are the weapon affixes that fire after a hit lands; `Building.damage_crate_at`/`_hit_poi`/`_hit_breakable`/`_hit_window` are the smashable world, and nothing in it breaks in one tap: each takes the swing's damage off a hit-point pool (`Breakables.HP`, `Buildings.CRATE_HP`/`WINDOW_HP`, `PointsOfInterest.CACHE_HP`), spends a `_prop_chip` puff while it holds (which also stamps `core.damage_fx` so the prop itself flinches and cracks), and only calls the matching `_break_*` (loot, debris, removal) on the blow that finishes it; `explode` is the powder keg going off, the one way to kill a crowd without a swing (damage falling off to the rim, the player included, chaining into any keg nearby, kills credited to whoever set it off); `_resolve_npc_hit` is also what calls `World.provoke_village`, so every path that lands on a villager (swing, arrow, cleave, blast) turns the settlement hostile the same way, and `_drop_villager_loot` is the purse a killed villager leaves (`loot.loot_villager`, a merchant's worth more); `_fire_ranged` spends the quiver in the player's ammo slot and falls back to the cheapest one carried when that slot is empty or spent, so choosing arrows never leaves a stocked player unable to shoot; `fire_monster_shots` is the ranged monsters' turn to shoot, spawning a hostile `Projectile` per archer/hexer in range on its own cooldown and only when `World.line_of_sight` says it can see the player and `Monster.cornered` says the player is not already on top of it, so breaking sight behind a wall and closing the gap are both real answers; `blocking_door`/`bash_doors`/`_hit_door` are a monster held up at a shut door beating it down, the one obstacle in the world it is allowed to break because it is the one that cannot be walked round (its own damage off `Buildings.DOOR_HP`, on `DOOR_BASH_COOLDOWN_MS`, and the hole it leaves is permanent); `update_projectiles` walks the in-flight arrows/bolts and delegates each to `_projectile_hits_monster` (used for both `monsters` and `bosses`, which take a hit identically) then `_projectile_hits_critter`/`_projectile_hits_npc`/`_projectile_hits_keg`, all of them finding what they struck through the one `_projectile_target`; an arrow is not choosy about whose it is, so a monster's shot that misses the player hits whatever is standing behind them, and everything that resolves that carries a `by_player` flag (False means no xp, no lootbox, no quest counter, no provoked village, no pack aggro: friendly fire kills, it does not pay); wildlife is hit on `Critter.hit_radius`, not on `size`, in both the swing and the projectile test
- `src/game/places.py`: `WorldPlaces`, mixed into `World`: what the player can do at a place once they have walked up to it, where `streaming.py` decides where that place is and when it exists. `_populate_camp` stands a bandit camp's surviving garrison up around its fire every time the chunk loads, rolling the size once on first sight and reading it from the camp's own `guards_alive`/`leader_alive` afterwards (each guard tagged with the camp's `camp_id`, which keeps it out of the roaming cap and out of the save), `on_guard_killed` strikes a fallen bandit off that count, which is the only thing that ever lowers it; a traveller camp instead gets its camper (once per save, `npc_spawned`). `camp_is_clear` gates both looting a bandit cache and resting: the garrison has to be `guards_defeated` *and* nothing hostile may be within `CAMP_CLEAR_RADIUS` right now, `camp_in_reach`/`rest_at_camp`/`rest_ready_in` are the fire the player rests at (a fraction of max HP back and the death debuff cleared, then that fire goes cold for `REST_COOLDOWN_S`, kept in `rest_cooldowns` and persisted so quitting can't reset it; that dict is keyed by POI id for a fire and by building id for a villager's bed, which `rest_in_house` puts on the same cooldown), `find_bandit_camp` looks for a camp still held for a clear_camp quest to name (loaded POIs first, then rings of chunks generated from their coordinates, so it can name one nobody has walked to) and `house_to_rob` picks the house a steal quest sends the player into, `provoke_village` turns a whole settlement hostile when one of its people is struck (affinity floored, quests dropped by the caller, nothing ever calms them, dogs included) while `theft_witness`/`catch_thief` are the smaller version of that for stealing: who can see the player from where they are standing right now (halved after dark, `Crime.WITNESS_RADIUS`), and that one villager alone turning on them, `unexplored_lead` is the nearest village site or POI the player hasn't walked to yet (asked chunk by chunk, each one seeing only the buildings around it), behind both `_traveller_directions` (a camper pointing the way) and the rumours the minimap marks (`mark_rumor`/`_clear_reached_rumors`, session-only and wiped once the player gets there), `_signpost_directions` is a signpost reading out that same lead and marking it like a rumour, `shrine_in_reach`/`pray_at_shrine` are the one prayer a shrine answers (persisted `prayed`, mostly a timed potion buff, `SHRINE_CURSE_CHANCE` of the time a curse: the Shaken weakness, a bite out of the purse or out of the health bar), `_reveal_around`/`is_explored` keep the explored-cell memory the minimap draws, and `_check_poi_discovery` fires a shrine saying what it is and what praying does, a quiet landmark saying what it is, or a camper's directions
- `src/game/streaming.py`: `WorldStreaming`, mixed into `World`: how the endless map appears around the player and what it gets called. `_sync_chunks`/`_load_chunk`/`_unload_chunk` generate and drop a chunk's floor details, wilderness (`game/entities/scenery.py`) and landmarks from its coordinates alone (`World.poi_state` holds the only part a player can change), and `_sync_chunks` ends by stepping the player out of anything a freshly generated chunk stood on top of them; `_reindex_scenery` rebuilds the per-chunk draw buckets and the collision grid once per sync, `_clear_scenery_for` cuts back the trees a new settlement was built in (its chunk's neighbours may have grown them first), `_ensure_village` builds the settlement a chunk's `village_site` offers, once ever, and keeps it in the save; `_generate_context` is the world lore streamed at first launch, `_start_village_naming`/`_start_landmark_naming` name what has been generated on background threads, `_check_village_discovery` announces a village once its name is ready
- `src/game/events.py`: `EventSystem`, random world events (merchant, treasure, blood night, rumours, village crisis); everything announces through `notify`, a method rather than the raw callback, because a presage waits on the LLM and can finish long after the player quit to the menu: it checks `world.closed` and drops the message rather than talking to a dead session's widgets. A rumour is a lead, not flavour: `_generate_rumor` asks `World.unexplored_lead` for somewhere the player has never walked, toasts the whisper and calls `World.mark_rumor` so the minimap points at it (a prophetic rumour marks the treasure it promised instead), and with nothing unexplored nearby the event simply passes
- `src/game/quest.py`: `Quest` dataclass (fetch/kill_mob/loot_mob/recover_stolen/slay_boss/clear_camp/steal/deliver types), to_dict/from_dict (de)serialisation; a slay_boss quest links to its boss by internal tag in `target_monster_kind` and shows `boss_name`, filled in by `World.sync_quest_boss_names` once naming finishes; a clear_camp quest names its camp by POI id (`target_poi_id`), a steal quest names the house by building id (`target_building_id`) and a deliver quest names the person by `recipient_npc_name`, with `target_x`/`target_y` written down for the two that point at a fixed place (a camp may sit in a chunk nobody has loaded). `COUNTED_QUEST_TYPES` is the one list of the types finished by a counter rather than by an item changing hands, read by the tracker, the dialogue prompt and the hand-in check alike
- `src/game/loot.py`: `open_lootbox` rolls coins/item from a lootbox rarity; `break_crate` rolls the smaller coins/common-item reward from a smashed shop or tavern crate (coins credited straight away, the item left for the caller to drop on the floor); `open_poi_cache` is the better-odds, properly rarity-rolled version for a wilderness ruins/camp POI; `loot_villager` is the purse and occasional possession a killed townsperson leaves (a merchant's is worth three times a labourer's), deliberately small: killing a villager should be a decision with a price, not an income; `roll_shop_stock` rolls a merchant's stock from the same tables when the LLM's list is unusable; all of them draw their item from `_roll_loot_item` (weapon/armor/accessory/ammo/potion, one shared name table)

### game/entities
- `src/game/entities/entities.py`: `Entity` base class (hp, damage, attack animation, `start_attack_anim(hand)` to force which arm swings), `draw_human` sprite renderer, which takes an optional `gear` dict and draws it on the body/hands before rotating the sprite
- `src/game/entities/gear.py`: drawing for the gear visibly worn by a character: a weapon per hand shaped by its archetype (dagger/sword/axe/hammer/spear/staff/bow), an offhand shield (`draw_shield`, carried at the arm's side and swung across the body while the block is up, the only in-world readout that the shield is raised), an armour ring on the body, an accessory gem, all in the sprite's local unrotated space so they turn with the facing and swing with the arm; only the player passes gear today
- `src/game/entities/player.py`: `Player(Entity)`, movement, inventory (`add_item` merges ammo and potion stacks by name *and* rarity, never across rarities: merging a legendary quiver into a common one used to throw the better item away), passive health regen held off until `Player.REGEN_DELAY_MS` after the last hit taken (`passive_regen_rate` is the delayed part, a regen potion is added on top and never delayed), potions and timed buffs (`use_potion` drinks one off a stack, `apply_buff`/`buff_magnitude`/`active_buffs` hold the wall-clock buff state read back by `damage_multiplier`/`speed_multiplier`/`receive_damage`, `quick_potions` lists what the HUD quick keys drink), equipment slots (`equip`/`is_upgrade`) split into separate melee and ranged weapon slots (a bow/staff and a sword can be equipped and carried at once, `_equip_slot` routes a weapon item by its archetype) plus an offhand shield, armor, accessory and ammo (the quiver `WorldCombat._fire_ranged` spends first; ammo is deliberately excluded from `is_upgrade`, since which arrows are loaded is a choice rather than a power level), the `weapon_bar` the number keys switch between (`c.Player.WEAPON_SLOTS` ids persisted under the `weapon_bar` save key, `select_weapon` routing a bar slot into the melee or ranged equip slot by archetype so switching a bow never puts the sword away, `_add_to_weapon_bar` filling it as weapons are equipped and `cycle_weapon_slot` the manual right-click assignment), the shield block (`has_shield`/`block_fraction`/`_update_guard`/`_blocks_hit`/`_absorb_with_shield`: hold Space to raise it, only hits arriving within `Shield.ARC_DEG` of the facing are cut, what is stopped drains the guard meter, and emptying it breaks the guard for `GUARD_BREAK_MS` while that hit lands in full), `effective_max_hp` (the post-death weakness takes a slice of max HP off on top of damage and speed), `apply_weakness` (the one timer behind that "Shaken" state, started by dying and by an angry shrine), affix-effect helpers (crit/lifesteal/burn/execute/thorns/dodge/regen-still/coinfind/xpgain/pierce) each taking a `ranged` flag to read the matching weapon slot, `heal`, `gain_coins` (coin-find), thorns/dodge and Guardian's Ward in `receive_damage` (the ward fires only on a hit that would actually kill, as its tooltip promises), `last_hit_by` (what last landed a blow, named by `_damage_source_name`, session-only and read once by the death screen), the spawn grace (`invuln_until`, session-only and never persisted, opened by `grant_spawn_grace` on entering the world and on respawning, checked first in `receive_damage` so nothing lands at all, spent early by `end_spawn_grace` the moment the player swings or shoots, and drawn as the pulsing ring `_draw_spawn_grace` puts round the body so its end is read rather than discovered), `gear()` describing the equipped items for `draw_human` to render on the character
- `src/game/entities/npcs.py`: `NPC(Entity)`, fights back once struck (`hostile`, set for a whole settlement by `World.provoke_village` and persisted: `_hunt` chases the player down and swings on `Entities.NPC_DAMAGE`/`NPC_ATTACK_COOLDOWN_MS`, `can_talk` shuts off dialogue, shops and prompts, and outrunning them by `NPC_HOSTILE_RANGE` sends them back to wandering their home, still furious); tracks per-NPC `affinity` (relationship level moved by concrete player actions, quest completion and shop trades, feeds dialogue tone/quest rewards/shop prices) through the single `AFFINITY_TIERS` ladder behind both `affinity_descriptor` (the prompt hint) and `affinity_tier_color` (the dot by their name); strolls around its `home` via a shared `Wander` (see `wander.py`) and pauses to face the player up close
- `src/game/entities/wander.py`: `Wander`, the idle-then-stroll movement shared by NPCs and critters: pick a random spot near an anchor, walk to it sliding along walls, idle a random delay, repeat. `step` returns the angle actually moved (or None) so each owner maps it onto its own sprite facing; `interrupt` drops the current target (used when a critter finishes fleeing)
- `src/game/entities/monsters.py`: `Monster(Entity)`, `pick_monster_kind` (spawn selection by distance from center), `apply_burn` + burn-tick state (weapon burn affix, ticked in `World.update`), `knockback_immune` (False here, True on `Boss`) and `camp_id`, the bandit camp a guard was posted at: a tagged monster never despawns and never counts toward the roaming population cap, so a camp can only be emptied by killing it; `move` takes an optional `waypoint` (from `World.chase_waypoint`) it steers to instead of the player while the attack swing still keys off the real distance, plus the world's `damage_mult` and `detection` for the moment (both raised at night, so a monster that spawned at noon is no gentler after dark) and the `crowd` it has to share the ground with; `_aim_point` is what it actually walks to, its own bearing (`slot_angle`, spread evenly across a pack on spawn) on a ring just inside its own reach rather than the player's exact position, and `_separate` shoulders it out of anything standing in the same spot, the two of them being what turns a chasing pack into a ring instead of one body; a `ranged` kind (Archer, Hexer) never closes: it holds `keep_distance`, backs off at `Entities.RETREAT_SPEED_MULT` (always slower than it advances) and leaves the shot itself to `WorldCombat.fire_monster_shots`, until `cornered` says the player is right on top of it, when it stops retreating, stops shooting and swings at `melee_reach`, so closing on an archer is a real answer rather than a stalemate; `_steer` probes a lookahead distance rather than one step so a monster turns away from a wall early instead of grinding into it, never probing past the goal itself (a wall behind the player is not in the way) and falling back to a short probe when every long one is blocked, which is what gets it into a corner or between two pieces of furniture; `_charge` is the Ogre's rush (plant, telegraph the locked-in lane with `_draw_charge_telegraph`, then cross the gap at `Charge.SPEED_MULT`, no special damage, the ordinary swing does that) and `_flank` is the Wraith's refusal to walk straight in (approach bent to one side, flipping every few seconds, fading out as it closes so it still arrives); a `group` kind (Goblin, Wolf) is rolled once and stood up as a pack by `World._spawn_monster_away_from`
- `src/game/entities/boss.py`: `Boss(Monster)`, a named LLM-titled boss with an enrage phase and telegraphed abilities (slam AoE, hostile bolt volley, summon adds), knockback immune; spawned at the landmark, by roaming, by events and by slay_boss quests
- `src/game/entities/village.py`: `Village`, `village_site`, `sites_near_chunk`, `generate_village`, `generate_starting_world`. Settlements are how buildings exist: a square region of chunks settles at most one of them (`_region_site`, a pure function of the coordinates, with a later region standing down when its site lands within `MIN_GAP` of an earlier one), and the cluster is laid out on a grid around an open plaza whose middle slot is left free for the well `Village.draw`/`Village.blocks` own. Unlike a POI a village is generated once and then saved (its NPCs carry names, affinity, quests and stock, none of which a chunk seed can rebuild): `village_site` says where, `World._ensure_village` builds it, the save keeps it. `village_site`/`_region_site` are cached (pure functions of their coordinates, asked over and over by chunk loading, POI placement and the roads), and `sites_near_chunk` is the list of sites around a chunk the roads are drawn between. `generate_starting_world` is the town the player opens in plus the ruined landmark placed alone in the settled ring
- `src/game/entities/buildings.py`: `Building`, `set_active_buildings`, one building's footprint, interior and drawing (placement belongs to `village.py`). A building's interior is its own footprint in world space (`interior_rect`), not a separate room: `blocks` collides against a thin wall shell (`_wall_segments`) whose door gap is filled by the door itself while it is shut plus furniture (`interior_layout`, deterministic per building id, whose `beds` list holds both a tavern's row of them and a house's single one, since the player can sleep in either), and `contains_point`/`draw(..., player_inside=True)` swap that one building to a roofless cutaway view when the player is standing in it, drawn by `_draw_interior`. Shop and tavern crates are breakable (`damage_crate_at` takes a swing's damage off a per-crate pool and reports `(rect, destroyed)`, with the pool session-only in `crate_hp` and only the finished ones saved in `broken_crates`, debris drawing); smashed crates can drop an item into per-building `dropped_items` (not persisted), picked up through `Game.current_interaction` (the interior draws the loot but no prompts, so only one prompt is ever on screen); `window_rects` gives the two windows flanking the door on every non-landmark building's facade, shatterable (`broken_windows` state, persisted). The front door is its own thing: shut until somebody opens it (`door_closed`, `toggle_door` off the player's E), a wall while it is, beaten down over several blows by a monster that cannot reach the player any other way (`damage_door`, session-only `door_hp` like a crate's, persisted `door_open`/`door_broken`), and drawn in whichever of the three states it is in by `_draw_door`, cracks and all, from the same rect the collision shell uses. `style()` is how a street stops being the same house repeated: rolled once from the building's id (so it survives a save) into a roof material and form (`_draw_roof`/`_draw_roof_texture`: thatch, tile, shingle or slate over a gable, hip or flat roof), a wall tint and one or two extras drawn by `_draw_extras` (a smoking chimney, a porch, shutters, a flower box, a woodpile). The kind still reads first: a shop keeps its awning and a tavern its sign whatever it is roofed with
- `src/game/entities/scenery.py`: `Scenery`, `generate_chunk_scenery`, `road_points_for_chunk`, `blocking_index`: the wilderness between the villages. A chunk rolls one biome (`Scenery.BIOME_WEIGHTS`) and lays down what that biome is made of in clumps, so a wood reads as a wood rather than as an even sprinkle: ground patches, ponds, grass, flowers, pebbles under the entities, trees/pines/boulders/stumps/reeds over them. Trunks and boulders are the only things that stop movement (`blocks`, reached through `World.blocked` via the fine grid `blocking_index` builds), and roads join each village site to its nearest neighbour, bent by a seeded sine and kept clear of anything solid. Streamed and dropped with its chunk exactly like the floor details, never saved, so nothing the player does can change it
- `src/game/entities/breakables.py`: `Breakable`, `generate_breakables`, outdoor props scattered near houses/shops/taverns (deterministic per building, weighted by kind): "barrel" gives the same coin/item odds as a shop crate; "powder" is a keg that pays nothing and detonates instead (`WorldCombat.explode`), the only prop an arrow can set off, drawn squat and iron-banded with a lit fuse so it is never mistaken for a barrel; "bush"/"flowerbed"/"herbs"/"sapling" are what is planted around a doorstep, pure decoration with no reward ("pot" is only drawn for saves made before the clay pots were replaced). Each carries persisted `hp` (`Breakables.HP`) so it takes several swings to clear, shows how far along it is as it goes (a hard prop cracks through `core.damage_fx`, a planted one loses bulk and blooms), and is gone for good once broken, no debris state (unlike interior crates)
- `src/game/entities/poi.py`: `PointOfInterest`, `pois_for_chunk`, wilderness landmarks generated per chunk from its coordinates (at most one, kept clear of the chunk's own edges, of buildings and of every village site nearby, generated or not) so the world is endless and walking out always finds something; a POI is fully described by its chunk seed apart from `state()`/`apply_state()` (looted, discovered, npc_spawned, guards_alive, leader_alive), which is all `World` saves, keyed by the POI's `"cx:cy"` id; "ruins" is a smashable loot cache (`looted` persisted, left in place afterwards, just visibly picked over) via `game.loot.open_poi_cache`, "shrine" says what it is and what praying does the first time it is walked up to (`discovered` persisted) and then answers one prayer, for good or ill (`prayed` persisted, no loot), and "camp" rolls a `variant` from its id: its cache takes several blows to force (`cache_hp`, session-only: a POI is rebuilt from its chunk seed anyway); a "bandit" camp is a garrison held as a count (`guards_alive`/`leader_alive`, `guard_killed`, `guards_defeated`) around a cache that only opens once they are dead (its tents are cut down and its banner drops when looted), a "traveller" camp is a camper who trades and gives directions. Both keep their fire lit whatever happens to them, because the fire is what the player comes back to rest at. Beside those three: "farmstead" is a second lootable cache (`has_loot`) with a barn and a fence of its own, "graveyard"/"watchtower"/"stones" are places rather than rewards, each saying what it is the first time it is reached (`PointsOfInterest.LANDMARK_MESSAGES`), and "signpost" reads out the way to somewhere never walked to and marks it on the minimap (`World._signpost_directions`)
- `src/game/entities/critter.py`: `Critter`, `pick_critter_kind` (species by distance from the world center, like a monster kind), every animal in the world. What one does with the player is entirely its `CritterKind.temperament`: "passive" (rabbit/deer/fox) only runs, "retaliate" (badger/boar) answers a blow and then breaks off at `BREAK_OFF_HP_FRAC`, "predator" (wild dog/bear) hunts on sight inside `detection`, "guard" (dog) is peaceful until its village or camp turns. `_should_hunt`/`_hunt` are the chase and the bite (contact damage on a cooldown, `lunge_until_ms` the only tell it came from the animal), `_fleeing`/`_flee` the running: a fleeing animal commits to a heading and bends it only `FLEE_TURN_RATE_DEG` a frame, sprints for its `stamina_ms` and then trots at `TIRED_MULT`, so catching one is a question of its wind rather than of out-turning a jitter. Huntable on a `hit_radius` scaled off its size rather than half of it, since none of them are drawn as a circle; killed in `WorldCombat._kill_critter` for a sellable pelt/tusk/hide. Session-only like particles or projectiles (never saved): wildlife is spawned/despawned around the player by `World`, in herds or packs (`CritterKind.group`), and dogs are stood back up from their village (`World._ensure_village_dogs`) or camp (`_populate_camp`) the way a garrison is. Drawn as a small ellipse-and-head or as a real quadruped (`_draw_quadruped`: flank polygon, legs, neck, plus per-kind antlers/tusks/ears/tail) because a plain ellipse with two lines off the head reads as a snail from a top-down camera, with a small health bar once wounded
- `src/game/entities/items.py`: `Item` (weapon/armor/shield/accessory/ammo/potion/misc; ammo and potions stack via `quantity`, misc are sellable "valuables" drawn as a coin; `SHIELD_KEYWORDS` is checked before the armour ones in `item_type_from_name`, so anything named a shield/buckler/targe is its own offhand item type rather than body armour, and its `bonus` doubles as flat defence and as how much it blocks), potions (`potion_effect_from_name` reads heal/regen/strength/swiftness/stoneskin out of the name, `potion_magnitude`/`potion_duration` scale it by rarity, `potion_description` writes the tooltip line, drawn as a "flask" coloured by effect), rarity rolling (`roll_rarity`, `rarity_tier`, `rarity_color`), `roll_bonus`, `roll_affixes`/`affix_label` (weapon/armor special effects stored in `Item.affixes`), expanded `ACCESSORY_FLAVORS` (+crit/lifesteal/coinfind/xpgain/pierce) with `ACCESSORY_FLAVOR_LABELS`, `base_value` (sell worth used by shop and inventory tooltip), `icon_shape` (the one place an item's icon is decided: a weapon takes its archetype's silhouette, dagger/sword/axe/hammer/spear/staff/bow, and body armour its own cuirass, so gear is told apart at a glance instead of every weapon being a generic sword and every plate a shield; the art itself lives in `item_icons.py`), `start_pop_anim` for a dropped item hopping out of its source and settling, `_draw_ground_marker` (shadow plus a halo pulsing in the item's rarity colour, drawn only for loot lying in the world, never for HUD/menu icons) with a slow bob so loot is spotted from across the screen instead of blending into the grass
- `src/game/entities/item_icons.py`: `draw_shape_with_border`, the vector art behind every item icon, one branch per shape `items.icon_shape` can return (weapon silhouettes, cuirass, shield, gem, arrow, flask, coin, chest). Kept apart from `items.py` so a search for what an item *is* never lands in a hundred lines of polygon coordinates; the HUD, the inventory, the shop and `gear.py` all draw through it
- `src/game/entities/projectile.py`: `Projectile` plus `ARROW_COLOR`/`BOLT_COLOR`, a fired arrow or magic bolt travelling in a straight line until it hits or runs out of range (`style`, `color`, `knockback`, `shake`, `hostile` for monster/boss bolts that damage the player, `pierce`/`hit_ids` for the arrow-pierce accessory, `owner_id` seeded into `hit_ids` so a shot can never strike its own shooter). `update` advances in hops no longer than the projectile is wide: a whole frame at once let an arrow cross a 16px wall whenever the framerate dipped
- `src/game/entities/stats.py`: `Stats` class, use-based character progression (xp, training, derived bonuses like attack/damage reduction/speed); `train` queues `pending_levelups` on every level gained, drained each frame by `Game._pop_levelups`

### llm
- `src/llm/llm_request_queue.py`: `LLMRequestQueue`, serialises all LLM calls onto a worker thread (`get_llm_tasks` is what the HUD spinner reads), served in priority order: the categories in `INTERACTIVE_CATEGORIES` (the dialogue the player is waiting on) go ahead of background work, so quest analysis queued as one conversation closes no longer holds up the next NPC's greeting; ties break on arrival. Use `generate_response_queued` / `generate_response_stream_queued` (both take `max_tokens`, the streaming one also takes extra `stop` strings). A streamed response is cut at its first line break, so the model carrying on past its reply never reaches the caller. `poll=True` is what the main thread must pass on a stream: it drains the chunk queue instead of waiting on it and yields `None` when there is nothing new, since a running call cannot be preempted and blocking there froze the game until it finished; `llm_busy()` says whether anything is running or queued at all, which is what stops the player opening a conversation on top of it
- `src/llm/dialogue_manager.py`: `DialogueManager`, manages NPC dialogue window (streaming, quest detection on close); `_draw_purse` puts the player's coins beside a merchant's Shop button, haggling being the one conversation where what's in the purse decides what to say next; replies are capped at `Hyperparameters.DIALOGUE_MAX_TOKENS` with `DIALOGUE_STOPS` ("Player:") so an NPC can't ramble or write the player's turn, and `_trim_to_sentence` cuts a capped reply back to its last finished sentence; the streams are polled (`poll=True`), so `update` returns on a `None` chunk and the game keeps drawing while the reply is written, and the input box takes nothing while a reply is still streaming; `END_RE` recognises the conversation-ending marker in the shapes the model actually writes ([END], bare uppercase END closing the reply, bolded, parenthesised) rather than only the literal "[END]"
- `src/llm/quest_system.py`: `QuestSystem`, analyses conversation for quests, creates items, handles completion/rewards; `analyze_conversation_for_quest` asks the model both whether the NPC offered a task and whether the player took it (`player_accepted`), since an offer the player turned down is not a quest; each type is built in its own branch of `create_quest_from_analysis` and finished by its own hook, `on_monster_killed`/`on_boss_killed`/`on_npc_killed` for the older ones and `on_camp_cleared` (a garrison emptied), `on_theft` (the named house's chest opened, which is when the stolen item is created) and `on_delivery` (the player walks up to the person a parcel is addressed to; the reward is still collected from whoever gave the quest) for the new ones, with `_pick_recipient` choosing the furthest NPC away so a delivery is a journey
- `src/llm/merchant_system.py`: `generate_shop_inventories`, stocks every merchant in one LLM call (one call for the whole town, not one per shop, which used to be the queue's biggest cost), each shop given a role (blacksmith/alchemist/general trader) so they differ; asks for compact `shop|name|type|rarity|price` lines and falls back to `loot.roll_shop_stock` for any shop the model fails to fill instead of retrying
- `src/llm/name_generator.py`: `NPCNameGenerator`, background-thread generation of NPC names ahead of time; persists the ready buffer and used-name history so a continued game reuses them instead of regenerating, and `close()` stops a name still in flight from writing into the next game's save
- `src/llm/death_taunts.py`: `DeathTauntGenerator`, the mocking line the death screen shows, written ahead of need on a background thread and buffered in the save (`Death.TAUNT_BUFFER`), exactly like the name generator and for the same reason: the death screen is blocking, so `take()` never waits and falls back to `Death.FALLBACK_TAUNTS` on an empty buffer. The taunts never name a killer, since they are written before anyone knows who it will be; `Game._respawn` puts `Player.last_hit_by` on its own line instead

### core
- `src/core/constants/`: all game constants, one flat namespace (`import core.constants as c`) split into six modules by subject, so a new constant is filed by what it tunes rather than appended to one long file: `ui.py` (screen size, `Minimap`, colours, `Fonts`, LLM `Hyperparameters`), `player.py` (`Player`, `Death`, `Stats`/`STAT_LABELS`, `Affinity`), `combat.py` (`Shield`, `Projectile`, `Combat`, `WeaponArchetype` + `weapon_archetype`, `Decals`), `bestiary.py` (`Entities`, monsters, `Charge`/`Flank`, bosses, critters, `Wildlife`), `world.py` (`World`, `Events`, `DayNight`, `Crime`, `Buildings`, `Breakables`, `Scenery`, `Villages`, `Fog`, `PointsOfInterest`) and `items.py` (`Rarity`, `LootBox`, `Potions`, `Affixes`). `Fonts` is the one name rebound at runtime (`__main__` swaps the class for a loaded instance), which works because everything reads it off the package. What is in them: LLM hyperparameters including the shorter `DIALOGUE_MAX_TOKENS` cap on NPC replies; `Shield` (block fraction per point of the shield's bonus, the frontal arc it covers, the guard meter and what breaking it costs); `Death` (coin loss, how long the post-death weakness cuts damage, speed and max HP, `SPAWN_GRACE_S` for how long nothing lands on a freshly spawned player, plus the death-screen taunt buffer and its canned fallbacks); `Crime` (how far a villager sees the player stealing, and how much of that is left after dark); `DayNight.NIGHT_*` (after dark: respawn rate, damage, detection range, the danger bonus new spawns roll with, and the roaming-boss multiplier); `Charge`/`Flank`, the rush and the circling approach behind `MonsterKind.charge`/`flank_deg`, and `World.PACK_SPREAD` for how far a pack kind is scattered on spawn; `WeaponArchetype` per-family combat feel (reach/swing/damage/cooldown/knockback/crit/cleave/shake, plus `min_hit_distance`, the polearm blind spot that makes the spear hit a ring instead of a disc so nothing pressed against the player is hit, and `arc_deg`, the wedge the swing both covers and draws) resolved by `weapon_archetype(name)`, plus `Combat` tuning (including the swing-arc look), `Explosion` (a powder keg's radius, falloff, what it costs the player and how far it chains) and `DamageFx` (how long a struck prop flinches, how many cracks a nearly-broken one carries); `Affixes` weapon/armor effect pools + rarity-scaled magnitudes and burn timing; `Potions` per-effect rarity-scaled magnitudes, buff durations, liquid colours and quickbar size; `BossKind`/`BOSS_KINDS` archetype templates (brute/warlock/colossus) and `Boss` tuning (enrage, abilities, rewards, spawn caps, health bar); `Scenery` the wilderness: what blocks and at what radius, the ground kinds and their draw order, the per-biome composition, ground-patch colours, clearances and the road tuning; `PointsOfInterest` per-chunk POI placement/loot tuning plus the shrine numbers (`SHRINE_MESSAGES`/`SHRINE_EXPLANATION`, `SHRINE_BLESSINGS`/`SHRINE_CURSES` and `SHRINE_CURSE_CHANCE`) plus the camp numbers (bandit-vs-traveller odds, guard count/spread/leader bonus, `CAMP_CLEAR_RADIUS`, `REST_DISTANCE`, camper stock and hint range); `Villages` settlement spacing (`REGION_CHUNKS`/`REGION_CHANCE`/`MIN_GAP`, deliberately wide: a village is meant to be a find, which only works because the wilderness between them has cover, landmarks and roads), grid slots, per-size composition and plaza/well look; `Fog` explored-cell size and reveal radius, `Minimap` size/range/palette plus the rumour mark and the day/night clock strip drawn under it; `World.PERSIST_MIN_INTERVAL_S`, the floor on how often a background thread may serialise the whole world to disk; `World.STEER_LOOKAHEAD`/`STEER_MIN_PROBE`/`STEER_CLOSE_PROBE`/`DOOR_APPROACH_DISTANCE` for monster navigation and `Entities.CHASE_RING_MARGIN`/`CHASE_ARRIVE`/`SEPARATION_PUSH`/`RETREAT_SPEED_MULT`/`CORNERED_FRAC`/`RANGED_MELEE_RANGE` for how a pack spreads round the player and how an archer behaves once caught, `Buildings.DOOR_HP`/`DOOR_BASH_REACH`/`DOOR_BASH_COOLDOWN_MS`/`DOOR_COLOR` for the front door and what it takes to break one, `World.BUILDING_INDEX_PAD` for the chunk-bucketed building lookup and `Player.PICKUP_DISTANCE` for how far loot is picked up from; `Entities.LOOT_*` for the ground halo and bob under dropped loot; `CritterKind`/`CRITTER_KINDS` one entry per animal (look, hp, `temperament`, `min_distance`, `group`, sprint/stamina, bite damage, drop) with `CRITTER_KINDS_BY_NAME` for the ones placed deliberately (the dog), and `Wildlife` the tuning they share: spawn/despawn distances, wander idle, the flee model (`FLEE_TURN_RATE_DEG`/`TIRED_MULT`/`RECOVER_MS`/`BOLT_DURATION_MS`), `BREAK_OFF_HP_FRAC`, `PACK_AGGRO_RADIUS` and how many dogs a village or camp keeps; `World.FREE_SPOT_MAX_RINGS`, how far `World.free_spot_near` searches for somewhere to stand, and `World.CAMP_SEARCH_RINGS`, how far `World.find_bandit_camp` generates outward looking for a camp to send a quest at; `Player.REGEN_RATE`/`REGEN_DELAY_MS` and `Stats.VITALITY_REGEN_PER_LEVEL`, kept deliberately low so healing up is a between-fights thing; `Decals` splat sizes plus the kill-spray fan tuning
- `src/core/save.py`: `SaveSystem`, atomic thread-safe JSON save system; background generators persist on completion via `save_all` (keys: `context`, `coins`, `weapon_bar` (the ids the number keys reach for, separate from `equipped`, which is what is drawn right now), `name_buffer`, `used_names`, `breakables`, `pois` (a dict of touched-POI state by id, not a list of POIs), `villages` (every settlement generated so far; its buildings and people live in `buildings`/`npcs`), `explored` (the "gx:gy" cells the minimap has lit), `camp_rest` (when each fire or villager's bed will serve the player again), `death_taunts` (death-screen lines written ahead of need), `buffs`, plus player/world state)
- `src/core/camera.py`: `Camera`, world to screen coordinate translation; `ScreenShake`/`get_shake` global camera-shake state applied in the translation
- `src/core/screen_fx.py`: `Hitstop`/`get_hitstop`, a brief freeze-frame (slows gameplay dt, not render dt) triggered on crits/kills/boss deaths; `HurtVignette`/`get_vignette`, a red screen-edge flash triggered when the player takes damage
- `src/core/damage_fx.py`: the half of a breakable's feedback that lives on the prop instead of in the air. `get_damage_fx()` is a session-only registry of recent blows keyed by a *string* (a crate or a window is an index into a building's layout, not an object), read back by the drawing code as a flinch `offset` away from the blow and a `flash`; `draw_cracks` is the wear itself, seeded from world space and growing as the hit-point pool empties, so how battered something looks is how close it is to giving. Called by `WorldCombat._prop_chip` on the way in and by `breakables.py`/`buildings.py`/`poi.py` on the way out
- `src/core/swing_arcs.py`: `SwingArc`/`SwingArcSystem`/`get_swings`, the trail a melee swing leaves, spawned by `WorldCombat.handle_attack` over exactly the wedge (`WeaponArchetype.arc_deg` at the weapon's reach) the hit test accepts, wider and hotter for a cleaving weapon. Session-only and updated in `Game.run` like particles and floating text; the picture and the hit test read the same two numbers, so the arc can never promise reach the swing does not have
- `src/core/daynight.py`: `DayNightCycle`, owned by `World` (`world.daynight`), tracks elapsed time within a repeating cycle and exposes `darkness`/`is_night`/`phase` (the "Day"/"Dusk"/"Night"/"Dawn" word the minimap's clock spells out); `draw` overlays an ambient night tint outdoors (only, drawn from `Game.run()` next to `get_vignette().draw`), overridden by a fixed dark red tint during blood night regardless of time of day; `is_night` also speeds up monster respawn in `World.update` (weaker than, and not stacked with, the blood night respawn multiplier)
- `src/core/decals.py`: `Decal`, `DecalSystem`/`get_decals`, capped session-only ground blood splats (small on a hit, bigger "pool" on a kill), drawn beneath entities in both outdoor and interior rendering; `spawn_spray` throws a fan of droplets out along the killing blow (thinning out with distance), used by `World._spill_blood` so a death leaves a directional mess instead of one tidy circle
- `src/core/floating_text.py`: `FloatingText`, `FloatingTextSystem`/`get_floating_text`, rising/fading damage numbers popping over a hit (bigger and gold on a crit)
- `src/core/utils.py`: `ConversationHistory`, random color/coordinate helpers, `parse_shop_inventories` / `parse_response_quest_analysis` (LLM response parsing; a response that won't parse ends as "no quest" and is recorded through `llm_log.log_parse_failure` rather than printed and lost). The quest parser repairs the model's near-JSON by quoting every unquoted value, so booleans arrive as text and go through `_as_bool`: plain `bool()` read `"false"` as true and handed over quests the player had refused
- `src/core/dialogue_log.py`: `write_conversation`, persists finished NPC conversations to Markdown files under `logs/dialogues/`
- `src/core/llm_log.py`: `log_call` and `log_parse_failure`, appending every LLM generation (any category, streaming or not) as a JSON line to `logs/llm_calls.jsonl`, with prompts, response, duration, and token counts, for later quality/speed analysis
- `src/core/particles.py`: `Particle`, `ParticleSystem`, world-space particle bursts for combat/pickup feedback; `spawn_burst` (omnidirectional) and `spawn_directional_burst` (cone away from an angle) both take an optional `gravity` (fake-z arc, so a particle launches up and settles back onto the ground plane instead of floating forever) and `shape` ("circle" or a rotating "shard", for wood/glass debris)
- `src/core/audio.py`: `SoundManager`, procedural sound effects synthesised in memory (no audio asset files)

### ui
- `src/ui/widgets.py`: shared menu/HUD draw primitives (flat square panels, buttons, slots, scaled item icons) plus `EQUIP_SLOTS`, the one definition of the equip slots as the UI shows them (slot key, caption, ghost glyph: melee, ranged, shield, armor, trinket, ammo), used by both the HUD strip and the inventory paper-doll, so adding a slot lands in both at once; `wrap_text` word-wraps and breaks a single word too long for the line so nothing runs off a panel; all menus and the HUD draw through these for one consistent dark theme
- `src/ui/game_renderer.py`: `GameRenderer`, draws the world, entities, camera-relative UI; `_draw_potion_bar` is the potion quickbar above the health bar (one slot per `Potions.QUICK_KEYS` letter, since the number row went to the weapon bar) with `_draw_buff_chips` showing the live buffs and their countdown over it (including the post-death "Shaken" weakness), `_draw_equipped` is the HUD paper-doll (returning the y its captions end at, so what follows doesn't guess) and `_draw_weapon_bar` the number-key weapon bar under it, gold-bordering whichever of its weapons are actually in the melee and ranged slots right now; `_draw_guard_bar` sits just under the health bar and only when a shield is carried, brightening while the block is up and turning red on a broken guard; dock icon tooltips are collected during the icon loop and drawn last in `draw_ui`, so the stat chips underneath can't cover them; `_hidden_indoors` skips any entity or item standing on another building's floor, which still has its roof on, so nothing indoors is drawn over the outside of a building; `_draw_interaction_prompt` draws the one prompt handed in by `Game.current_interaction`, and `draw_offscreen_indicators` now points at the tracked quest's target only (pointing at every dropped item and every boss made the screen edge unreadable); `draw_world` pulls buildings from `World.buildings_in_range` rather than the whole list, draws the wilderness in two passes around them (`World.scenery_ground_in_range` under the entities with the floor, `scenery_props_in_range` with the barrels) and draws each village's plaza under them, `draw_ui` owns the `Minimap` in the top right corner (the LLM spinner moved left to make room)
- `src/ui/minimap.py`: `Minimap`, the small top right map. It draws memory, not perception: explored cells (`World.explored`), the buildings/villages/POIs standing on them, a facing arrow for the player, plus the rumour marks (`World.rumor_marks`) that are the one thing on it the player has not walked to, pinned to the panel edge when they lie further out than `Minimap.RANGE`. Under the panel sit two strips drawn by `_draw_strips`: the current village's name and `_draw_clock`, the day/night dial (hand swept by `DayNightCycle.progress`, marker warm by day and cold by night, the phase spelled out beside it), which is drawn even when M has toggled the map itself off. Deliberately no monsters, NPCs, loot or quest marker, and a fixed zoom of about 1.5 screens, so it orients without scouting
- `src/ui/conversation_ui.py`: `ConversationUI`, dialogue text box rendering, scrolling, text input; the box is `BOX_HEIGHT` tall with a header/footer split (`HEADER_HEIGHT`/`FOOTER_HEIGHT`) giving ~10 visible lines, `scroll` is driven by both arrow keys and the mouse wheel, and a hint is drawn in whichever direction has text off screen; `close_button_rect` is the cross in the box's top right corner, hit-tested by `DialogueManager` (a merchant's Shop button is placed just left of it)
- `src/ui/notification.py`: `ToastNotification`, on-screen popups; word-wrapped onto a few lines (`MAX_WIDTH_FRAC`) instead of one line running off the screen, since a rumour or a landmark's explanation is a full sentence and the toast is the only place it is shown
- `src/ui/quest_tracker.py`: `QuestTracker`, permanent HUD widget below the minimap on the right showing one tracked quest in full plus swap chips for the rest, collapsible; owned by `DialogueManager` (`dialogue_manager.quest_tracker`), replaces the old slide-in "new quest" banner
- `src/ui/loading_indicator.py`: `LoadingIndicator`, spinner shown while the LLM is generating

- `src/ui/menus/context_menu.py`: `ContextMenu(BaseMenu)`, streaming popup for ambient/context LLM text
- `src/ui/menus/inventory_menu.py`: `InventoryMenu(BaseMenu)`, item list, equip/unequip, clicking a potion drinks it, right-clicking a weapon walks it along the number-key bar (`Player.cycle_weapon_slot`, the bar slot shown as a corner number on the cell); `_grouped_items` sorts the grid by `TYPE_ORDER` then rarity and bonus, so gear arrives in blocks with the best of each at the top rather than in pickup order; the hover tooltip is drawn on the screen after the panel, flipped and clamped to the screen edges, so it overhangs the panel instead of being clipped by it
- `src/ui/menus/shop_menu.py`: `ShopMenu(BaseMenu)`, `_sell_price`, buy/sell UI and pricing; `_valuables`/`_unused_gear` back the two bulk-sell buttons under the sell column (`_sell_all` runs the batch through the ordinary `_sell` so bartering xp and affinity are still credited per item, and `_unused_gear` spares anything equipped or held on the weapon bar, as well as ammo and potions entirely) (bartering stat and NPC affinity both swing prices); a stack (ammo, potions) sells one unit per click; each column scrolls on its own (`buy_scroll`/`sell_scroll`, wheel or arrows over that column, scrollbar when the list is longer than `_visible_rows`), ESC or B closes
- `src/ui/menus/quest_menu.py`: `QuestMenu(BaseMenu)`, active/completed quest list; `_draw_card` stacks name, description, objective and reward, with the objective/reward block pinned to the card bottom and the description trimmed to the lines that fit above it
- `src/ui/menus/stats_menu.py`: `StatsMenu(BaseMenu)`, character stats/progression display
- `src/ui/menus/help_menu.py`: `HelpMenu(BaseMenu)`, controls/help screen. `CONTROLS` is the only written record of the key map, so a rebinding that misses it leaves the game lying to the player
- `src/ui/menus/game_over.py`: `run_game_over`, death screen. Not a menu: it holds for `Death.RESPAWN_DELAY_S`, names what killed the player, mocks them with the taunt `Game._respawn` hands it, reports the penalty, swallows input and returns so `Game._respawn` puts the player back at world spawn

## Architecture notes

- The LLM runs on a background thread via `LLMRequestQueue`. Never call `llama_cpp` directly from the main thread.
- Healing is scarce on purpose, and each source has one job: potions mid-fight, a campfire for a partial patch-up once per fire per cooldown, a bed for a genuine full rest, and a slow passive trickle that only starts `Player.REGEN_DELAY_MS` after the last hit. Anything that hands back full health for free (the campfire used to) collapses the whole difficulty curve, so new healing goes through one of those four rather than beside them. A bed comes in two forms and they cost different things: the inn's takes coins, a villager's own takes the risk of being seen and puts that household on the same `REST_COOLDOWN_S` a campfire gets, so a street of houses is not a row of free full heals.
- The spawn point is protected ground, and it is protected in three ways at once because any one of them alone still gets the player killed on arrival. Nothing hostile is *spawned* within `World.SAFE_RADIUS` of the world centre (`_spawn_is_sheltered`, which also covers every settlement plus a margin), so the starting town has no ring of monsters waiting one screen out; the player is *placed* by `World.safe_spot_near`, which looks for hostiles as well as walls, with `clear_hostiles_around` first sending whatever chased them there back out; and they arrive with `Player.invuln_until` open for `Death.SPAWN_GRACE_S`, spent early the moment they attack. How crowded the world is at all follows from where the player is standing (`World.roaming_cap`), not from a world-wide constant: the ground around the starting town is deliberately thin and the deep wilds are full. Monsters may still *wander* into any of this; what is forbidden is being stood up there.
- Night is a state of the world, not a property of what spawned in it. `World.night_damage_mult` and the detection multiplier are read per frame and passed into `Monster.move`, so a monster that spawned at noon still hits like a night monster after dark; only the danger bonus a new spawn rolls with is baked in at spawn time.
- An animal's behaviour is its species' `temperament`, nothing else. `CritterKind.temperament` ("passive"/"retaliate"/"predator"/"guard") is the single switch behind whether a `Critter` runs, fights back, hunts on sight or takes its village's side, and `Critter._should_hunt` is the one place that reads it. Adding an animal means adding a row to `CRITTER_KINDS`, not a subclass and not a new branch in `World.update`. Dogs are that table's one deliberately placed kind (weight 0): a village or camp stands them up and owns them, which is why they carry `village_key`/`camp_id` while wildlife carries neither.
- Violence against a villager is a whole-settlement event. Any blow landing on an NPC goes through `WorldCombat._resolve_npc_hit`, which calls `World.provoke_village`: every NPC inside that village's radius turns hostile at once, their affinity floors and their quests are dropped. There is deliberately no path back to friendly. Theft is the one exception to the all-or-nothing rule and it has exactly one entry point of its own: `Game._check_witness` asks `World.theft_witness` who could see the player empty a chest or climb into a bed, and `World.catch_thief` turns that one villager, alone, while the rest of the settlement goes on with its day. Escalation is the player's own doing: swinging back at whoever caught them lands in `_resolve_npc_hit` like any other blow and turns the whole village. No other path may turn a single NPC hostile. The second exception is not the player's doing at all: a monster's stray arrow can kill a villager, and `_resolve_npc_hit(by_player=False)` skips the provocation, because the village has nothing to blame the player for.
- Nothing the player did not do pays the player. Friendly fire (a monster's arrow catching another monster, an animal or a villager) resolves through the same `_resolve_*_hit` methods with `by_player=False`, and that flag is the single switch for every reward and consequence attached to a kill: xp, hitstop, lootbox, quest counters, pack aggro, the village turning, a villager's purse. The kill itself always happens; only the credit is withheld. Two things deliberately survive an uncredited kill, because they are world state rather than reward: a bandit camp's garrison count (`on_guard_killed`) and the body's dropped item, which is lying on the ground for anyone to take.
- Nothing in the world breaks in a single hit. Crates, barrels, pots, bushes, windows, doors and wilderness caches all take the swing's damage off a hit-point pool and only give on the blow that empties it. Where that pool lives follows what the thing already saves: a `Breakable` persists its own `hp`, a building's crates/windows keep theirs session-only (the save records what broke, not how far along the rest are), and a POI's `cache_hp` is session-only because a POI is rebuilt from its chunk seed anyway. The pool is never invisible: every one of them draws its own wear through `core/damage_fx.py`, so a prop that is nearly gone looks nearly gone, and a blow that did not finish something still visibly landed on it.
- A door is the one obstacle a monster may break, because it is the one that cannot be walked round. Every door starts shut and is part of the wall shell while it is (`Building._wall_segments`), the player opens and shuts it with E, and a chaser that ends up on the wrong side of one either lets itself in (a villager, `World.open_door_for`: it is their street) or beats it down (`WorldCombat.bash_doors`, several swings, audible, permanent once through). Nothing else about the world may become breakable to let a monster take a shortcut: if a monster cannot reach the player, the answer is navigation, not demolition.
- The one exception to "everything breakable is loot or scenery" is the powder keg, and it is there to answer a different question: how do you kill a crowd without a better weapon? `WorldCombat.explode` hurts everything near it, the player included, chains into other kegs, and pays nothing at all, so a keg is a piece of ground worth fighting over rather than another barrel. It is also the only prop a projectile interacts with: shooting one from across a clearing is the plan the mechanic exists for.
- `World` is one class split across four files by mixin: `world.py` owns the state, `combat.py` (`WorldCombat`) resolves blows, `streaming.py` (`WorldStreaming`) builds the map, `places.py` (`WorldPlaces`) is what happens at a place the player has walked to (camps, fires, shrines, directions, theft, the explored-cell memory). They all work on the same `self.monsters`/`self.items`/`self.pois`, which is the point: the entity lists stay a single source of truth, and the file you open is decided by what you are changing, not by which object holds the data. Adding a method means picking the file by that same rule, not defaulting to `world.py`.
- `src/` is the package root; all imports are relative to it (e.g. `import core.constants as c`).
- No tests exist; skip the pre-push hook accordingly.
- Don't launch the game (`uv run game`, or any script that opens a pygame window) to verify a change, and don't ask Valentin to launch it and report back. It pops a real window on his live desktop. State what changed and stop; he'll test it himself if he wants to.
- To self-check a rendering/UI change, a throwaway script that imports just the drawing code, stubs out heavy objects, and renders to an offscreen `Surface`/PNG is fine, but set `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy` before `pygame.init()` in it. Without that, `pygame.display.set_mode(...)` pops a real window on Valentin's desktop too, same problem as launching the game.
- A building's interior is just its own footprint in world space, not a separate coordinate space or screen: `Building.interior_rect()` is the footprint inset by `Buildings.WALL_THICKNESS`, and `Building.blocks()` collides against a thin wall shell (with a permanent door-sized gap) plus furniture, instead of treating the whole footprint as one solid block. `Game.interior` is recomputed every frame from the player's position (`world.building_at`) rather than tracked as an enter/exit mode switch; there is no teleport, no separate indoor monster/projectile lists, and `World.update`/`World.handle_attack` always run against the one `world.monsters`/`world.items`/`world.projectiles`. `GameRenderer.draw_world` draws every building normally except the one the player is standing in, which `Building.draw(..., player_inside=True)` renders as a roofless cutaway (floor, walls, furniture) so the rest of the map keeps drawing around it in the same pass.
- Item persistence works by id: `SaveSystem` stores `inventory`/`equipped` as item id lists and `items` as the one master list of full item dicts (see the key list in `src/core/save.py`). Anything handed to the player has to end up in `world.items` or its id won't resolve on reload and the item silently vanishes. For items that are granted instantly (chest, lootbox) rather than picked up off the ground, they're appended already flagged `picked_up = True`, purely so the id resolves later; they never render or get picked up again.
- `Building.dropped_items` (loot popped from a smashed crate, waiting to be picked up) is session-only and not written to the save file. A `Building` object itself lives for the whole process, so e.g. dropped crate loot survives leaving and re-entering the room, but not a save/reload.
- The world has no edge. `World.WORLD_SIZE` is only the settled area: it fixes the starting village, the spawn point, boss roaming ranges and the distance-from-center that scales monster difficulty. Past it the map keeps going, and points of interest, villages and floor details all stream in per chunk, so walking out never runs into a wall of nothing. Because a POI is regenerated from its chunk seed on every visit, it must stay deterministic from `(cx, cy)` alone: anything the player changes goes in `World.poi_state`, keyed by the POI id, and nothing else about a POI is saved.
- Villages are the exception to that rule, deliberately. `village_site(cx, cy)` (where a settlement goes) is a pure function of the coordinates like everything else streamed, but the settlement itself is generated **once** and then persisted with the world, buildings in `buildings` and people in `npcs`, exactly like the starting town. Regenerating it per visit would mean rebuilding NPC names, affinity, quests and LLM shop stock from a seed, which is impossible. So: the map is endless and deterministic, what stands on it is generated on demand and kept. New villages must go through `World._ensure_village` so their buildings reach the chunk index (`_index_buildings`) and `set_active_buildings`.
- The wilderness is terrain, not entities. Everything in `scenery.py` (trees, boulders, grass, ponds, roads, ground patches) is generated from its chunk's coordinates, held only while that chunk is loaded and never saved, exactly like the floor details: nothing the player does can change a single piece of it, so there is no state to persist and no id to key. A chunk rolls **one biome** and lays out that biome's clumps, which is what makes a wood a wood; scattering every kind evenly over every chunk gives texture, not places. The only part that touches gameplay is collision: trunks and boulders answer `World.blocked` through their own fine grid (`Scenery.INDEX_CELL`, finer than the chunk grid the buildings use, because a wood holds far more trunks than a village holds houses), which is enough for the player, monsters, critters, arrows and spawning all at once, since they already go through `blocked`. Monsters get round a trunk by the lookahead in `Monster._steer`; `World._detour_corner` stays about buildings only, a tree being too small to be worth a detour.
- Nothing walks the whole building list per frame. Buildings and village wells are bucketed by chunk (`World._index_buildings`), and collision (`blocked`, `building_at`), swing/window tests, chase detours and the renderer all go through `buildings_near`/`buildings_in_range`. The list only grows as the player finds villages, so a full scan would make the game slower the more of the world had been seen.
- Exactly one interaction prompt is on screen at a time. `Game.current_interaction` picks the nearest interactable in reach (world loot, NPC, campfire, door, interior dropped item/chest/bed), `Game._interact` runs that same one, and `GameRenderer._draw_interaction_prompt` draws it. Never draw a prompt from the entity/building that owns the thing: that is what used to stack a label over every bed in the tavern, and what let a prompt point at something other than what the key did.
- The weapon bar sits on top of the equip slots, it does not replace them. `Player.weapon_bar` is a short list of weapon ids the number keys reach for; pressing one calls `select_weapon`, which routes that weapon into the melee or the ranged equip slot according to its archetype. Combat never asks which weapon is "active": left click still swings `melee_weapon` and right click still fires `ranged_weapon`, exactly as before the bar existed. So switching a bow leaves the sword equipped, and nothing in `combat.py` had to learn about the bar.
- An item's icon is derived, never stored as a decision. `items.icon_shape(item_type, name)` is the single source of the shape, `Item.__init__` sets it from there and `Item.from_dict` recomputes it rather than restoring the saved value, so an old save picks up new artwork instead of keeping a stale silhouette. Nothing about an item's identity or behaviour lives in `shape`.
- Particles, floating damage numbers and blood decals are each one global session-only system (`get_particles`/`get_floating_text`/`get_decals`), updated once per frame in `Game.run()` rather than inside `World.update`, so they keep animating even while a menu pauses the rest of the update. They're drawn from `GameRenderer.draw_world`, same call whether the player is indoors or out.
- A bandit camp's garrison is a number, not a set of monsters. `PointOfInterest.guards_alive`/`leader_alive` are persisted with the camp; `_populate_camp` stands that many bandits up when the chunk loads, `_unload_chunk` takes them away with it, `World.serialize` never saves them, and `on_guard_killed` is the only thing that lowers the count. So a camp costs the same whether the player has found one or a hundred, killing four of five guards survives walking away and reloading, and clearing a camp is recorded rather than inferred from who happens to be loaded. Guards are tagged `Monster.camp_id` (with `camp_leader` for the one worth remembering), which is what keeps them out of the distance despawn, out of the roaming population cap and out of the save. They come back at full health, which is the one thing the count deliberately forgets.
- The map is memory, not radar. `World.explored` is the only thing that lights the minimap up, it is filled in by walking (`_reveal_around`) and persisted, and the minimap draws no live entity of any kind at a fixed small zoom. Anything that would let the player scout from the HUD instead of walking (monster dots, a full-screen map, zoom) is a deliberate omission, not a missing feature. The single exception is a rumour's mark (`World.mark_rumor`/`rumor_marks`): somewhere the player was *told* about, held session-only and rubbed out on arrival, which is what turns a rumour into somewhere to go. It marks a place, never a creature, and nothing else may put a pin on the map.
- `Hitstop` (`core/screen_fx.py`) only slows the `dt` fed to gameplay updates (player/monster movement, `World.update`, projectiles); camera shake, particles, floating text and decals keep advancing on the real `dt` so the freeze reads as a snap rather than the whole frame stalling.
